# recworker.py — the recorder as a unit on every server: М10's `RecWorker`, unchanged

**Role in the module.** Lessons 2 and 4. `ClusterRecorder(RecWorker)` is what `worker.py` is for the worker: М10's class by the name the lessons use, with the `env=` calling convention. Its unit (`deploy/cluster/systemd/vms-recworker.service`) hands it `WORKER_NAME` (`r-%l-1`, claimed by CAS on `rec/slots/*`), `SERVER_NAME`, `LABELS`, `CAPACITY`, `BOX_ID` (the machine id: a network volume's hold follows the name at once only on the same box), `OBSD_SOCKET` (`/run/vms-obsd/obsd.sock`). It takes a VOLUME — a declared one it may serve, by CAS on `rec/holds/*`, or its server's own, `ARCHIVE_VOLUME` or `vms.config.OWN_VOLUME` (`/data/vms/obsd/volume`), which `__main__.recorder` says — and opens it through the host's `obsd`. It holds no camera: each recording's pipeline subscribes to the worker's tee from the VMS heartbeat — `live_shm` (shared memory under `/run/vms`) when the worker is on the same server, `live_url` (RTSP) otherwise — and writes the stream `<recording>/e<epoch>` into its volume. It serves that volume at its archive door (`ARCHIVE_HOST:ARCHIVE_PORT`, announced as `ARCHIVE_URL`).

`between` says `WATCHDOG=1` at every turn of its loop, as the worker's (`cluster.worker.notify`). When its camera's holder is not heard — its server's door away — the store says whether it still holds the camera (`RecWorker._held_by_the_book`; the twelfth review, major 10).
