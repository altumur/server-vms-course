# P/06.9 — Group operations: norms

This English text is the source; the Russian translation `ru/spec/06.9-console.norms.md` (for people, not in the export) is derived from it by translation and is not edited by hand.

The canon of chapter 6.9 of lesson P/06 «Cluster console» (the Cluster console). Lecture (`ru/06-console.md`, for people, not in the export), [contract](06-console.api.openapi.yaml),
[instructions](06-console.README.md), [life cycle](../lifecycle.md), [acceptance](../acceptance/9/README.md).

A group operation is a console record that lives longer than a request: acceptance answers 202 at once, the steps go later one by one. The console
runs the operation: it keeps the rows, holds the turn and the generation, issues the ticket, puts it on pause. The subsystem translates and writes a step: only it knows what
the body of a step means. Therefore the console parses neither the body nor the root of the target: the mechanism suits any subsystem.

The chapter stands on norms introduced earlier: the batch and the replay memory — [chapter 6.1](06.1-console.norms.md), request parsing and the common codes — [chapter 6.5](06.5-console.norms.md),
sealing — [chapter 6.2](06.2-console.norms.md), the turn — [the writer turn contract](../../common/contracts/turn.md). A reference «chapter 6.K» is a chapter of this lesson. Notes.
The request ticket, the transport of the step call and the callback list are the gate-and-pass contract, «Pass of the second kind», [chapter 6.3](06.3-console.norms.md) N1.9. Norm ids are `P/06.9-MM`.

## N1 Form

### N1.1 Declaration in the manifest

<!-- req: P/06.9-R003 -->

A subsystem declares operations with the key `door.pass.operations`, next to `calls` ([P/02.1](../../02-installer/spec/02.1-installer.norms.md) N1.2): the route of the step call, its own limits and whether the table is a service one.

```yaml
door:
  pass:
    routes: [...]
    calls:  [<s>/operations, ...]      # doors of operations by the request ticket (chapter 6.3 N1.9)
    operations:
      step:    <step call route>       # does not lie under routes and does not coincide with them by the first segment (relation 132): unreachable for the browser
      limits:  {steps?, active?, per_person?, parallel?}   # not above the ceilings of N1.6
      service: true | false            # the operations table is a service one, like the table of P/06.1 N1.6 `service`; default false
```

Without the declaration there are no doors: `404 no operations`. The names of the families are closed by the loader against `unit.rows` and tables ([P/02.1](../../02-installer/spec/02.1-installer.norms.md) N1.2a), the patterns of the role `console` are given only by the declaration ([P/02.5](../../02-installer/spec/02.5-installer.norms.md) N1.4).

### N1.2 Operation rows

<!-- req: P/06.9-R004 -->

All rows are written by the console: the outcome and the counters come to it in the step batch from the subsystem, but it is the door that writes them into the store.

```yaml
<sub>/operations/<id>:   {by, jti, kid, iat, exp, created_at, finished_at?, state: running | done, paused, expired, cancel, total, retry_of?}
<sub>/opsteps/<id>/<i>:  {body, root, expected, password_secret?}
<sub>/opdone/<id>/<i>:   {state: ok | fail | cancel, code?, field?, version?, created_id?, versions?}
<sub>/opcount/<id>:      {done, ok, failed, cancelled}
<sub>/opbusy/<root>:   {operation}
<sub>/operations-turn:   {holder}
<sub>/operations-epoch:  {n}
```

- `<id>` — 128 random bits, 32 lowercase hex. It is named by the caller of the door «accept»: the caller's memory row carries the operation id and lands in the same batch, so the caller needs the id before the batch.
- `<i>` — the step number from zero, decimal, without leading zeros.
- `by` — the author: the steps go by his right. `jti`, `kid`, `iat`, `exp` — the token to which the operation is bound now; `iat` is the input of step 4a of the right contract on the step callbacks: a token older than the assignment `admin` does not serve step 4a.
- `state` in the row is only `running` or `done`. The doors give out `paused` if not `done` and `paused` or `expired` is set.
- `body` — the step body without secrets. The sealing contract encrypts only the fields `*_secret`, so the secret lies in a separate field: the subsystem takes it out of the body at acceptance, the console seals it into `password_secret`
  (AAD path `/password_secret`) and substitutes it only into the step call. There is one such value per step.
- The counters are a row of their own: they are written by the step batch with `cas`. The steps of one operation go strictly one by one, so the `cas` of the counters does not contend with itself, and the progress stream reads one row instead of all the outcomes.

### N1.3 Target root and occupancy

<!-- req: P/06.9-R005 -->

Different targets share store rows: at a subsystem one version can be glued together from several rows. Therefore at acceptance the subsystem names the **root** of every step. A root is an opaque string: one component of a key, not `.`, not `..`,
at most 200 bytes of UTF-8. The console compares roots only for equality.

`<sub>/opbusy/<root>` — who holds the root. The row is created by the acceptance batch `cas ""` and removed by the finishing batch. The root of an unfinished operation is busy, on pause included. Acceptances without common roots do not contend: the rows differ. `BUSY` is judged only by acceptance and retry:
single edits of an object in an operation are not refused, they are guarded by `If-Match`, and a step after a foreign edit gets a version mismatch.

### N1.4 Expected version of a step

<!-- req: P/06.9-R006 -->

The value `expected` of a step is opaque JSON: the subsystem puts into it the expected versions of the objects of the root. Into the step call the console passes one value, expected_version(i):

1. Let j be the largest number < i that has the same root and the outcome ok.
2. If j exists, the value is versions of the outcome j.
3. Otherwise the value is expected of the row of step i.

After a refusal without a write the expected version does not change: the outcome `fail` does not enter the chain. The leader does not re-read a foreign new version as the person's consent: a foreign edit gives the step `fail` and does not pass silently. `version` at a creation step is the version of the target after the step; the id of the created object is `created_id`.

Vectors — [`op-step`](../vectors/op-step/README.md).

### N1.5 Step outcome

<!-- req: P/06.9-R007 -->

- `ok`, `fail` — the outcome of a step. The outcome is written by the subsystem: with a write — by the step batch, with a refusal without a write — by a batch of two rows, the outcome and the counters. `code` is its code; the console does not parse it.
- `fail` with the code `not_run` is written by the console: the call exhausted `OPS_STEP_TRIES`, the value of the secret did not open, the step row is unreadable.
- `cancel` is written by the console at finishing: `cas ""`, so a late batch of the former leader runs into a ready outcome.
- `versions` is mandatory at `ok`: without it the next step over the root would not get an expected version.

Reconciliation of the outcomes with the counters: `done = ok + failed + cancelled`, at finishing `done = total`.

### N1.6 Constants

<!-- req: P/06.9-R008 -->

The constants `OPS_STEPS_MAX`, `OPS_ACTIVE_MAX`, `OPS_PER_PERSON_MAX`, `OPS_PARALLEL`, `OPS_STEP_WAIT`, `OPS_STEP_TRIES`, `OPS_TICK`, `OPS_RUN_MAX`, `OPS_KEEP`, `OPS_REAP_EVERY` are declared by [forms/runtime-constants.tsv](../forms/runtime-constants.tsv); the meaning and the dependent bounds remain this section.

`OPS_STEPS_MAX` holds the weight of the acceptance batch. The acceptance batch is an internal write of the console: `N` occupancy rows and three more (the record, the counters, the caller's memory), `N + 3` in all. It is limited by the weight (the configured `-max-body` of the store, declared by its daemon-flags form, [P/01.3](../../01-store/spec/01.3-store.norms.md)), not by `BATCH_WRITES`.
The occupancy-row weight follows from the key and value forms of N1.2; at most `OPS_STEPS_MAX` distinct roots contribute such rows to the batch. The weight of the memory row is held by the ceiling of the subsystem's table. The finishing batch removes the same `N` rows and writes up to `N` outcomes `cancel`: it is of the same order.

## N2 Doors

<!-- req: P/06.9-R009 -->

The doors always name the subsystem, like `/{sub}/requests`: an operation belongs to the subsystem that declared it. `backend` calls them by the request ticket: the entry `<s>/operations` of the list ([chapter 6.3](06.3-console.norms.md) N1.9). Request parsing — chapter 6.5 N2 «Console request parsing»; there are
no deviations of its own. A refusal — the form and the rules of chapter 6.1 N2 «Refusal: `error` and `field`», the words — [`refusal-words.tsv`](../data/refusal-words.tsv), the door `operations`. The contract — [OpenAPI](06-console.api.openapi.yaml), the paths `/{sub}/operations…`.

| Door | Body | Success | Right |
|---|---|---|---|
| `POST /{sub}/operations` | `OperationInput` | 202 `Operation`, no outcomes: `state` of the steps — the rule below | `edit` on the operations table |
| `GET /{sub}/operations/<id>` | none | 200 `Operation` | `view` on the operations table |
| `POST /{sub}/operations/<id>/pause` | none | 200 `Operation` | `edit` |
| `POST /{sub}/operations/<id>/resume` | none | 200 `Operation` | `edit` |
| `POST /{sub}/operations/<id>/cancel` | none | 200 `Operation` | `edit` |
| `POST /{sub}/operations/<id>/retry-failed` | `{id, memory?}` | 202 `Operation` of the new operation | `edit` |

There is no door listing operations: `GET /{sub}/operations` — `405 method`. There is no rollback door: rollback is not declared, `…/revert` — `404 no such path`. The reason: the «before» of a step with a secret would require either an open secret in the store or a write of a sealed value past the door.

```yaml
OperationInput:
  id:       32 lowercase hex                 # 128 random bits of the caller
  items:                                     # 1 … OPS_STEPS_MAX, the order matters
    - body:     <JSON>                       # the step body without secrets
      root:     <root>
      expected: <JSON>                       # the expected versions of the objects of the root
      secret:   <row>                     # optional; the value of the secret, open
  memory:                                    # optional; the caller's memory row, lands in the acceptance batch
    key:    <sub>/<table>/<name>
    fields: {…}                              # the POST body of this row of the table
    cas:    <index>                         # its row «started» (started) as read
Operation:
  {id, by, state: running | paused | done, created_at, finished_at, retry_of, total, done, ok, failed, cancelled,
   items: [{state: wait | run | ok | fail | cancel, code?, field?, version?, created_id?, body?}]}
```

`items[].body` — the step body without secrets, only with a declaration with `service: true`: the addressee wrote it itself, and the subsystem's memory row dies before the operation. Other declarations have no such field: reading an operation does not reveal the body.

A subsystem of the catalogue without a declaration answers `404 no operations` on all paths of the operation doors and all methods: it has no doors. Bodies are closed. `finished_at`, `retry_of` — `null` while there is no value. `state` of a step: there is an outcome — the outcome; there is no outcome and it is the first without an outcome of an unfinished operation not on pause — `run`; otherwise `wait`. `secret` does not go out in an answer; the value is stored open nowhere. The doors
do not read `Idempotency-Key`: the caller's memory is the field `memory`. Another method — `405 method`; a longer path — `404 no such path`; `<id>` not of 32 lowercase hex — `404 no such operation`.

The guard lets any person with a grant through to the doors: `403` and `404` are decided by the door, because `404` also hides the existence of a foreign operation.

- no `view` on the operations table — `404 no such operation`;
- `view` is there, `edit` is not — `403 denied` at `pause`, `resume`, `cancel`, `retry-failed`;
- no `edit` at acceptance — `403 denied`.

**Service operations table** (`service: true`). The rules above are those of a table without `service`. With `service: true` the doors go only by a live
request ticket from the leaf of this subsystem's role (the gate-and-pass contract, `service`); through the door of a person without a ticket they are closed, `403 denied`, like the table of P/06.1 N1.6 `service`. The right on the table is not judged:
acceptance goes by the right of consequences, the person's right is judged on the targets of the steps. A person sees only their own operation: `by` of the record ≠ `user` of the ticket — `404 no such operation` on reading and control, a foreign operation is as if
it did not exist. The translation introduces no rows `sub: <s>` on the table.

The steps go by the right of the author `by`: the right with which the operation was accepted decides nothing about its steps. Every step callback checks the fresh `may` of the author on every row of the batch.

**Step call** — `POST /<step>` of the console to an instance of the subsystem by the route of the declaration, the header `X-W2C-Pass-Ticket` — the step ticket, the body `{id, index, body, secret?, expected, opcount}`: the operation id, the step number from zero as an integer, the step body, the substituted value of the secret, the expected version, the index of the row `<sub>/opcount/<id>` as a string, like `cas` — the `cas` of this row in the step batch. The console does not read the answer body. The transport, headers, the answer type and the network timeouts are as at the pass of the gate-and-pass contract «Pass of the second kind»: the same path, the same `X-W2C-Pass-Ticket`, the console gives the worker's answer to no one; the deadline is `OPS_STEP_WAIT`. The address and the callback listener — there too.

**Server leaf.** On a stand with TLS the console checks the server leaf in the step call: the subsystem role and the instance are the addressee of the ticket. A foreign leaf — no call, neither the body nor the value of the secret goes out. The leaf check is the role certificate contract, `peer`.

**Step ticket** — a ticket of the gate-and-pass contract «Pass of the second kind», the form `ticket_form`. The console remembers `{user: by, jti, kid, iat, exp, operation, step, target instance, generation index}` (operation, step, addressee instance, generation index); the ticket lives while the step call is open. A callback passes the order `ticket` of the contract: the transport and the role leaf, the kind `person`, the list `calls` of the addressee, the fresh `may`, the revocation of `jti`, the deadline `exp`,
the key `kid`. In place of «an external person's request is open» (an external request of the person is open) stands «a step call is open» (a step call is open). There is no exception for the deadline: the deadline has passed — `401`, the operation goes on pause (N3 «step»).

**Step batch** — `POST /console/batch` by the step ticket. Beyond the checks of the batch door:

- `writes` carries exactly one row `<sub>/opdone/<id>/<i>` of its own step `cas ""` and the row `<sub>/opcount/<id>` with `cas`. A row of a foreign step or a foreign operation — `403 denied`: the ticket gives a write of only these two rows;
- rows of other families of the subsystem — by the author's right, as at the batch door;
- the console itself adds the generation guard `{key: <sub>/operations-epoch, index: ticket index}` (the index of the ticket);
- `Idempotency-Key` of a success batch is `step_key(id, i)`, so a lost answer to the same batch gets the saved answer of the memory, not a false `412`; a refusal batch (the outcome `fail`) goes without a key: a double outcome is cut off by `cas ""` of the outcome row.

```
step_key(id, i) = hex(SHA-256(UTF-8(id) ‖ 0x00 ‖ UTF-8(decimal notation of i)))     # 64 lowercase characters
```

A refusal by the generation guard is `412 leader moved`, a word separate from `row moved`: the subsystem ends the call and does not write the outcome `fail`. It is not an outcome of the step, so it does not go into the memory of `step_key`: it arises after the claim, at step 5 of the door's procedure (reconciliation by read) or at step 9 (`412 guard` of the store, [chapter 6.1](06.1-console.norms.md) «batch»), and there the door removes its `pending`; the target is reliably not performed. A call with the same `step_key` from a new leader goes through the procedure anew. At `412 row moved` by the guard of its own parts the subsystem re-reads the outcome: the outcome is there — the step is over, another executor wrote it; it is not — the outcome `fail` with its version mismatch code.

The feed `/changes` covers the record and the outcomes of the operation with the ordinary filter `view` ([chapter 6.1](06.1-console.norms.md) N2 «Change feed»); with `service: true` only the feed by the ticket, and only the author's operations: `by` of the record equals `user` of the ticket, foreign ones do not come. The subsystem translates the progress into the stream; the order of steps in the stream beyond the feed is not promised, the truth is `GET`.

### accept(sub, body) → 202 | refusal

<!-- req: P/06.9-R012 -->

1. If the manifest has not declared operations, return `404 no operations`.
2. Form: if the body is not a JSON object, return `400 not_json`; if the fields, id, items, root or memory are not in form, return `400 bad operation`, field.
3. Right (N2): at a table without service — `edit` on the table, at service — a live ticket from the leaf of the subsystem's role. If the session is not a person with a token (no jti), return `403 denied`: the steps go by the person's right, and without a token there is nothing to bind.
4. If items exceed the limit, return `400 too many steps`, field `/items`.
5. List the unfinished operations. If there are ≥ OPS_ACTIVE_MAX of them, or the author has ≥ OPS_PER_PERSON_MAX, return `400 too many operations`, field `/items`; if the read is not confirmed, return `503 unavailable`.
   The limit is judged by the read, not by the batch: two simultaneous acceptances can exceed it by one.
6. Write the step rows `cas ""` in order; the secret is sealed by chapter 6.2 with the key of this row.
   On conflict: if the operation record exists, return `409 exists` and do not touch the step rows; if there is no record (the tail of a break), return `503 store unavailable`; the bodies without a record will be removed by the reaper.
7. The acceptance batch, the order of writes:
   - memory, if any, with its cas (the check of the table row is its door, the right is the rule of the table);
   - the record `{by, jti, kid, iat, exp, created_at, state: running, paused: false, expired: false, cancel: false, total}` `cas ""`;
   - the counters at zero `cas ""`;
   - `<sub>/opbusy/<root>` `{operation: id}` `cas ""` for every distinct root.
8. When it is done: the journal `operation.accepted {id, by, total}`, return 202.
   - On 409 conflict on the record, return `409 exists`; remove the step rows by cas.
   - On 409 conflict on memory, return `412 row moved` {key, index}, field `/memory/cas`; remove the step rows: another executor of the same key has already accepted, the caller reads the memory.
   - On 409 conflict on opbusy, read the row; return `409 root busy` {operation, root}, field `/items/<i>/root`; remove the step rows.
   - If removing the step rows failed, or on a break, the reaper will remove them: the refusal does not depend on it.
   - On unavailable, ambiguous or no answer, return `503 store unavailable`; do not remove the step rows: the batch may have landed.

The reconciliation of `expectedVersions` is made by the subsystem before the call of the door: it is its knowledge. Between the reconciliation and the acceptance batch there is a window: if the target changed in it — 202 all the same, and the step then gets `fail` by a version mismatch. This is a limitation; it is written in the lecture.

The memory row goes in the batch by the same technique as a row of the memory table in the step batch: a lost 202 on a repeat finds the memory and gives the same operation; the steps are not executed twice.

### leader

<!-- req: P/06.9-R013 -->

One console per subsystem leads; the turn row is `<sub>/operations-turn` ([the writer turn contract](../../common/contracts/turn.md), `TURN_TERM`, `TURN_RENEW`). Every console tries to take the turn if the subsystem has unfinished operations.

1. Take the turn by the contract, holder — a new unique mark of the attempt. If it is not obtained, do not lead this tick.
2. Right after taking it, rewrite `<sub>/operations-epoch`: `{n: read + 1}` with cas of the read version (no row — `cas ""`, unreadable — cas of its version), under the turn guard.
   On no answer or conflict, consider the turn lost: this is the barrier of the contract.
3. Every OPS_TICK: list the unfinished operations, go through «round», lead at most OPS_PARALLEL operations.
4. The leader's writes (the flags of the record, the failure outcomes, the removal of the secret, the finishing batch) are under the turn guard; renewal is between these writes, not in parallel with them.
5. If there are no unfinished operations, give the turn back by step 6 of the contract; on SIGTERM, give it back.

The generation is the index of the epoch row, not the value `n`. The ticket remembers the index; the step batch guards it. Renewal of the turn does not change the generation, so parallel steps and renewals do not contend: the step call goes without a write of the leader, and the contract forbids renewal only in parallel with a target write.

### round (leader, every tick, all unfinished operations, paused ones included)

<!-- req: P/06.9-R014 -->

1. If jti is revoked (`domain/revoked`), by is disabled (`domain/disabled`, the read condition — the right contract, input `disabled`), kid is out of the accepted set `domain/keys` or its until has passed, or now > created_at + OPS_RUN_MAX, write `cancel: true` with cas.
2. If a read of a source is not confirmed, this sign is unknown: do not cancel, check at the next tick.
3. If cancel is set and there is no open step call of this operation, «finish».

The cancel is written into the row, so it is not lost when the revocation record goes away by `exp + 60`. A revocation that came while the console was unavailable longer than this term does not lose force: by that time the operation is on pause by `exp` and will continue only by a request of the author with a new token.

### step(operation)

<!-- req: P/06.9-R015 -->

1. If cancel, go to the round; if paused or expired, wait.
2. If now ≥ exp, write `expired: true` with cas; wait for the author.
3. Let i be the first number without an outcome; if there is none, «finish».
4. If the outcome is there, the step is over: at ok remove password_secret (cas of the step row); the next step is at the next tick.
5. If the step row is unreadable, or password_secret is there and did not open, write the outcome fail not_run (below) and go on.
6. The value of the expected version is by N1.4; if the outcome of the former step over the root is unreadable — the outcome of the family opdone.
7. The instance of the subsystem is the live holder of the route; the step ticket; the step call with the deadline OPS_STEP_WAIT; the journal `operation.step {user: by, via: <sub>/<instance>, op: <id>/<i>}`.
8. When the call is over, re-read the outcome. If it is there, go to step 4.
   If it is not: failures in a row + 1, the series `<sub>_op_step_retries_total` + 1; the next tick — with the same step_key, also to another instance.
   At OPS_STEP_TRIES failures: the outcome fail not_run, the series `<sub>_op_step_poisoned_total` + 1, the operation goes on.

The outcome fail not_run:

1. A batch under the turn guard: `<sub>/opdone/<id>/<i>` `{state: fail, code: not_run}` `cas ""`; the counters with cas.
2. On conflict on the outcome, the outcome has managed to land: go to step 4.

The steps of one operation go strictly one by one: the next call starts only when the previous one is over and its outcome has been read. A poisonous step does not hold the roots until `OPS_RUN_MAX`: after `OPS_STEP_TRIES` the operation goes on. Failures in a row are counted in the leader's memory: a new turn holder counts anew; this gives
up to `OPS_STEP_TRIES` more calls with the same key, but not an extra step.

A step whose fresh `may` has already refused is ended by the subsystem with the outcome `fail` with its own rights refusal code. The remaining steps at disabling — `cancel` (the round).

### finish(operation)

<!-- req: P/06.9-R016 -->

1. Condition: there is no open step call of this operation, or its ticket is dead.
2. A batch under the turn guard:
   - the record: state: done, finished_at: now, cas of its version;
   - every step without an outcome: `{state: cancel}` `cas ""`;
   - the counters with cas;
   - `<sub>/opbusy/<root>` of every root of the operation where operation = id: delete with cas.
3. When it is done, remove password_secret at every step with the outcome ok and cancel where it has remained (cas of the step row); at a step fail the sealed secret lies until OPS_KEEP: the retry needs it.
   A break is completed by the operations reaper; the journal `operation.finished {id, ok, failed, cancelled}`.
   - On conflict on an outcome, a late step batch has landed earlier: re-read and assemble the batch anew.
   - On 412, the turn is lost: the new leader will complete it.

The outcomes `cancel` land `cas ""`, so a late batch of the former leader runs into them if the generation guard has not cut it off. If the leader fell, the operation is picked up by the new turn holder; the occupancy is removed all the same.

### pause, resume, cancel

<!-- req: P/06.9-R017 -->

1. The form of the path; the manifest; the row of the record: absent, unreadable → 404, 503 (the outcome of the family).
2. Right (N2).
3. If state: done, return `409 operation done`.
4. Pause: `paused: true`; resume: `paused: false`; cancel: `cancel: true`; a request of the author is also a rebinding. If the value is already such and there is no rebinding, return 200 without a write.
5. Write the record with cas; on conflict, retry from step 1 at most OPS_CONTROL_RETRIES times after the original pass; after these retries return `503 store unavailable`.

The current step plays out: the flag is read before the next step. `cancel` also cancels waiting on pause. A human pause is not removed by rebinding: `expired` is removed, `paused` remains — the operation waits.

### rebind

<!-- req: P/06.9-R018 -->

A request of the **author** to the operation (read, `pause`, `resume`, `cancel`) in a session of a person with a token replaces in the record `jti`, `kid`, `iat`, `exp` with the current ones and removes `expired`, if they differ and the operation is not finished. The write is with `cas`; the journal `operation.rebound {id, was: <former jti>, now: <new jti>}`. A conflict at a read does not refuse
the answer: the rebinding repeats with the next request. A request not of the author does not rebind. The progress stream of the subsystem rebinds likewise: it is a request of the author by a ticket.

While the author's tab is open, the operation goes. Closed — it goes until the deadline of the last token and waits for the author.

### retry(sub, id, body) → 202 | refusal

<!-- req: P/06.9-R019 -->

1. Form; manifest; the row of the record; right — as at pause. If the session is not a person with a token (no jti), return `403 denied`: the record carries jti, kid, iat, exp of the one who retries.
2. If the operation is not done, return `409 operation running`.
3. Take the steps of the former operation with the outcome fail, in order; if there are none, return `409 nothing to retry`.
4. A new operation: the author is the one who retries; retry_of: id; the step rows are copies of body and root; open the value of the secret and seal it anew: the AAD contains the key of the row. If it did not open, the former envelope goes into the row as is, and step 5 of the procedure gives the outcome fail not_run.
   expected of a copy := expected_version(i) in the former operation (N1.4), that is, the version at the moment of its execution.
5. Further — «accept» from step 4: occupancy, limits, memory.

A changed step expectedVersion is reconciled by that step and is not a retry-admission `412`. The separate memory-row CAS of the acceptance batch still gives the existing `412 row moved` with key/index and field `/memory/cas`; no acceptance effects land, and step rows are cleaned up by the acceptance procedure. A matching memory CAS permits 202; an unknown acceptance outcome retains the existing pending/cleanup rules. If the root has changed, the step is again `fail` by a version mismatch, and the person builds a new plan. Inside the new operation the chain of N1.4 acts as usual: a successful step over the root gives the version to the next one.

### operations reaper

<!-- req: P/06.9-R020 -->

A new job of the console reaper ([chapter 6.4](06.4-console.norms.md) N3): every `OPS_REAP_EVERY`, outside the maintenance window. `cas` does not let two consoles remove one thing twice.

0. If the record is done and a step with the outcome ok or cancel still has password_secret, remove it (cas of the step row).
1. If the record is done and now ≥ finished_at + OPS_KEEP, remove the steps, the outcomes, the counters and the record — the record last: a break in the middle leaves the work to the next pass.
2. Step rows without a record, older than OPS_RUN_MAX by the first observation of this console: remove them.
3. An opbusy row whose record is done or absent (a confirmed read): remove it with cas.
4. An unknown read or an unreadable row: leave it.

### resealing

<!-- req: P/06.9-R021 -->

The rows `<sub>/opsteps/*` enter the round «reseal at start» of [chapter 6.2](06.2-console.norms.md) N3: otherwise, after a change of the ring and the withdrawal of the former `kid`, the body of an unfinished operation will not open. If the value did not open during the round, the row remains and the reason goes to the journal; the step will give `fail not_run`.

**Races.**
- Two acceptances with a common root: `cas ""` lets one through, the second gets `409 root busy`, nothing is written. Without common roots both pass.
- Two executors of one memory key: `cas` of the memory row lets one through, the second reads the outcome of the memory — the same operation.
- The leader is on a process pause, another one has taken the turn, rewritten the generation and finished the operation by cancel: the late batch of the former call is refused by the generation guard, the outcome remains `cancel`.
- A restart of the leader in the middle of a step: the new holder reads the outcome. It is there — the step is over. It is not — a call with the same `step_key`: a batch that has landed gives the saved answer, not `412`. The batch landed between the read of the outcome and the read of `opcount`: the new call has another `cas`, but `cas` is outside the batch fingerprint (chapter 6.1 N3 «replay memory»): the same row values give the saved answer — the step has landed, the outcome is right; other values of `opcount` give `422 key reused`; in both cases there is no second count, the subsystem ends the call, step 8 reads the outcome, there is no extra step.
- Two instances of the subsystem execute one step: `cas ""` of the outcome lets one batch through.
- A pause in the middle of a step: the step plays out, the next one is not started.
- A request of the author and the leader's write about `expired`: `cas`, the loser re-reads. A rebinding after `expired: true` removes it.
- A revocation on pause: the round writes `cancel`, so `resume` after `exp + 60` sees a finished operation — `409 operation done`.
- The token deadline and the call: `exp` came in the middle of a call — the callback gets `401`, the call ends without an outcome, the leader writes `expired`. A rebinding returns the operation to the same step with the same key.
