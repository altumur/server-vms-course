**Data-directory ownership.** A live daemon exclusively owns its `-dir` while its process runs. Acquisition is atomic: among simultaneous starts on one directory, including an empty one, exactly one becomes owner. Ownership is checked first among refusal reasons that depend on the directory. A second daemon uses `start_data_dir_owned`, separately from `start_format`: refuse before readiness and before any write, including `FORMAT`; name the directory in the log, leave the first daemon and its data untouched, and do not take over its ownership. Offline `import` and `restore` on a box, and local `recover`, use `local_data_dir_owned` on a live-owned directory: refuse without changing data and name the directory in the log. The existing in-process group import through its owning leader remains governed by `admin_ops`. Ownership ends with the process, on orderly exit and on abnormal termination including `SIGKILL`: former ownership itself MUST NOT cause the next start to refuse or require manual ownership-mark cleanup. Other existing refusal reasons, including `start_format` and, on a box, unfinished `import`/`restore`, remain. The positive restart is tested on a healthy completed state; unfinished transfer on a box is a separate negative case. The mechanism is declared in item 6 of «Implementation decides».

Fixed `DOOR_*` and `RAFT_*` numeric limits are owned only by [runtime-constants.tsv](../forms/runtime-constants.tsv). The effective `-leader-wait` and connection-limit defaults are owned by [daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv).

<!-- req: P/01.3-R032 -->
**Metric presentation (C01-BOX-OBSERVATIONS-01).** `configstore_raft_state` is a gauge: the current state has value 1; other state samples, when emitted, have value 0. The label is lowercase (`leader`, `follower`, `candidate`, `shutdown`), as in sc. 17; `Status.state` keeps its separate Leader/Follower/Candidate/Shutdown/Door spelling. Emitting only the current-state sample or also zero-valued other states is an implementation choice. A box emits `state="leader"` with value 1. A door without a vote keeps its own series and emits no Raft series, as above. `# TYPE` metadata is optional; when present, `configstore_raft_elections_total` and `configstore_door_forwards_total` have type counter, the other listed series type gauge. The existing series names, label escaping and required observations remain unchanged.

**Flag purposes.** The closed set, types, defaults, requiredness, commands and refusal predicates are sourced only by [forms/daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv). An unknown name is the `start_flags` refusal of the [refusal catalogue](../forms/refusals.tsv); the purposes below are not a second grammar.

- `-dir`: the data directory of the daemon; the mark `FORMAT` (`format` above); also of `recover`, `restore`, `import` — they write into the directory of a stopped daemon on a box (ADR-0125 addendum (2) item 1)
- `-id`: the member name in `members`, `voters`, `leader_id`; also of `recover`
- `-sockets`: the socket directory, the sockets in it flat (`sockets` above); the commands `status`, `join`, `leave`, `admit`, `backup`, `restore`, `import`, `rights` find `admin.sock` by it, `recover` does not (ADR-0125 addendum (2) item 1)
- `-rights`: the rights file
- `-tls`: the root of the certificate kits (N1.6), mTLS of `-raft` and `-api`
- `-raft`: the raft address that the daemon names to its partners
- `-raft-bind`: the address on which the daemon listens for raft, if it differs from the named one
- `-api`: the address of the service doors of N2 for partners; the value of `members` and `leader` in `/v1/status`; without `-api` the member is not in `members` (the map can be `{}`; C01-STATUS-MEMBERS-NOAPI, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-26)
- `-bootstrap`: the first server of the group (`join` above); with `-join` — a start refusal
- `-join`: the `-api` address of a group member through which the daemon joins (`join` above); always with `-tls`
- `-group-size`: the declared group size, the same on all servers
- `-force`: joining at a transitional evenness (`join` above), also of the command `join`; for `restore`, `import`, `recover` — only the clock refusal
- `-isolated`: only for `recover`, required: the operator confirms that the former group and the writers are isolated and nothing was handed out above upper (N3 step 0); without it `recover` is a refusal before changes (guess of stage C S02, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-23)
- `-upper`: only for `recover`: the bound of the lost tail of epochs known to the operator; it only raises the computed upper (`floor.generation`): upper := max(computed, N); N below the computed one — a refusal before changes (guess of stage C S02, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-23)
- `-peers`: only for `recover`: the recovered membership — the remaining members, the one being recovered (`-id`) among them; the id and both addresses are required for each: raft — the address of the raft membership, api — the value of `members` in `/v1/status`; any other notation — a refusal before changes (guess of stage C S03, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-23)
- `-door`: a door without a vote (`door` above)
- `-voters`: the voters for a door without a vote: the id and the `-api` address
- `-tuning`: the raft timing profile (`tuning` above)
- `-leader-wait`: the leader wait (`leader_wait` above)
- `-max-value`: the row ceiling (chapter 1.1 N4, `{row_ceiling}` of «Stand»)
- `-max-body`: the effective entity-body read limit of `POST /v1/write` and service POSTs of N2 (chapter 1.1 N2, `{body_limit}` of «Stand»), separately from the row ceiling `-max-value` (log of cammeta-python, lesson 11 № 24)
- `-max-conns`, `-api-conns`, `-api-per-address`: connection limits (below)
- `-index-base`: for `import`: the version base (`import` above)
- `-groups`: only for `rights` (`admin_ops` above; ADR-0125 addendum (2) item 1)
- `--from`: only for `restore`, `import`: the input — the backup copy (`copy` above; `admin_ops` above; ADR-0125 addendum (2) item 1)

The connection-limit defaults are owned only by their rows of [daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv), not by a hidden choice of each implementation. A deployment MAY set other positive limits before
the run starts and name them in the description; the connection limit gives 503 `unavailable` with `Retry-After: 1` (chapter 1.1 N4). `admin.sock` is outside these ordinary limits and has its own limit declared by the implementation (item 8 of «Implementation decides»), so the operator can enter when they are exhausted (ADR-0130 Addendum (13) item 11). ## N3 Procedures

### recover the store (no majority)

<!-- req: P/01.3-R020 -->
0. One operator isolates all former daemons and writers (including disconnected nodes, against a later return);
   saves the original directories/copy, checks upper and the whole input. The confirmation of isolation is the flag `-isolated` of the command `recover`:
   without it — a refusal before changes, code ≠ 0; a bound of the lost tail known to the operator is `-upper N`, it only raises
   the computed upper, below the computed one — a refusal before changes (guess of stage C S02, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-23); mutual exclusion of runs of recover/restore/import
   is item 6 of «Implementation decides».

<!-- req: P/01.3-R021 -->
1. On one stopped daemon: `configstore recover -id <me> -dir … -peers <remaining> -isolated [-upper N] [-force on a clock going back]`
   → the dump and the log are rewritten, the rows of the absent members are removed, `platform/epoch_floor` is raised by a generation.
   After all checks and the acquisition of ownership, `recover` writes a phase mark into `-dir` as its first changing step, with a barrier, and removes it as its last step, after the barriers of the result. The implementation does not destroy the input until completion, so a repeat runs from the same input; a partial dump gives no upper bound (ADR-0130 Addendum (15) item 2). While the mark stands, a daemon start refuses with code 2 (`refuse_start` of N1.3), and a repeat of `recover` starts over (ADR-0130 Addendum (13) item 9). After a partial `import`, `recover` cancels the unfinished attempt: the floor F2 that `recover` publishes becomes effective, a repeat of the former attempt with its floor F1 refuses, and the floor never decreases. The full input is transferred by a new `import` attempt; writers stay held until it completes (ADR-0130 Addendum (14) item 7).

<!-- req: P/01.3-R022 -->
2. Start only the declared recovered membership; the others join one by one from new empty directories
   after the old ones are saved (the second/fourth — with `-force` at a transitional evenness, not beyond the declared size).

<!-- req: P/01.3-R023 -->
3. Those gone forever — `leave` at the leader; those who return after `leave` — `admit`.

<!-- req: P/01.3-R024 -->
4. Processes that read another revision of the manifest are restarted (P/02.3); workers read the floor at the lease step (P/03.2 N3 «lease_step()»): a unit with
   an epoch below the floor loses its lease and is started anew above the floor (P/03.2) — a restart of processes is not needed for this.

<!-- req: P/01.3-R025 -->
**Races: admittance and recovery transitions.** A manual action by itself does not exclude two operators. In the lesson only one attempt per installation is allowed, with a confirmed cut-off of the former group; concurrent recover/restore/import are forbidden.

### Snapshot output and orderly stop

<!-- req: P/01.3-R034 -->
A copy MUST pin one immutable version of the state. Blocking external output MUST run outside the lock that serializes ordinary mutations. The implementation declares finite bounds for lock wait, output, cancellation and orderly stop; a stalled receiver MUST NOT retain the mutation lock until transmission finishes. Success, failure and cancellation release snapshot and transport ownership. An unrelated operation may keep its existing bounded pre-effect refusal; that response alone does not prove correct lock scope.

On stop, cease admitting new work. A request that is read but not yet admitted at that moment gets 503 `unavailable`; connections whose request has not been read are closed (ADR-0130 Addendum (13) item 9). Cancel or bound external I/O, and resolve or classify admitted work by its existing definite/ambiguous outcomes. Retire owned snapshots, connections and listeners within the declared orderly-stop bound. This bound MUST fit below the actual service-manager stop deadline with a separately measured teardown margin. The manager's old example is not a Go Shutdown budget; forced kill is not orderly success. Raft Barrier is not evidence of a storage durability barrier, and a Raft Shutdown return is not proof that the platform has drained its work. Import's complete-input check, exact versions and floor/progress phases remain unchanged; construction obeys [P/01.1 Persistence cost](01.1-store.norms.md#persistence-cost).

Caller action: another base / fix the copy.
- `clock_early`: `recover`/`restore`: a clock earlier than `generation_at`. Caller action: `-force` removes only the clock refusal; the upper bound/isolation/monotonicity are required.
- `recovery_unsafe`: recovery: upper unknown, the old group not cut off, an overflow. Caller action: restore the preconditions; force does not help.
- `recover_isolated`: `recover` without `-isolated` (N3 step 0; guess of stage C S02, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-23). Caller action: isolate the former group and the writers, repeat with `-isolated`.
- `recover_upper`: `recover -upper N`: N is out of the flag form or below the computed upper. Caller action: check the bound; `-upper` only raises.
- `recover_peers`: `recover -peers`: an element not `id@<raft host:port>/<api host:port>`, an empty element, a repeated id or no `-id` among them (N1.3 «Daemon flags» `-peers`; guess of stage C S03). Caller action: fix the list.


- `copy_method`: unsupported method at /v1/copy. Caller action: use GET; HEAD remains bodyless.
- `copy_admin_only`: copy requested through an ordinary role socket or forwarding. Caller action: use the authorized local admin socket; no header grants it.
- `copy_snapshot_unavailable`: a consistent copy cannot be obtained before successful headers within the declared wait. Caller action: retry the read when available; no write outcome is ambiguous.

- `start_data_dir_owned`: a second daemon on a live-owned data directory (N1.3 «Data-directory ownership»). Caller action: do not take ownership; retry only after the owner exits.
- `local_data_dir_owned`: offline box import/restore or local recover on a live-owned directory (N1.3). Caller action: stop the owner first and retain the operation's other preconditions.
- `replay_limit`: a write above the declared replay-processing limit (R035), before admission; `Retry-After` is the time until admission in seconds, computed by the implementation. Caller action: repeat not before that deadline.

## Implementation decides

Each implementation names in its description:

1. **the raft transport secret** beyond mTLS, if there is one, and its rotation on revocation; the raft wire between members and the frame of `admin.sock`
   (read-fence, admit, import) remain internal to the implementation: a format with a name and a version, no new HTTP routes. The copy transfer itself uses the explicitly declared GET /v1/copy of N2; the members of one
   store cluster are one implementation, a mix of languages — only by clients through `configstore:///` (guess of stage C CL-01,
   ADR-0087 addendum (12) item 7);
2. **the durability of the store** (fsync, raft snapshots: threshold, interval, tail) — a teaching store MAY be non-durable, and this is said in its description (ADR-0070 item 4); a stand flag that turns the barriers off is in the «Stand» command, not in the environment (N1.3 `durability`);
3. **the connection accounting of the daemon** and overrides of the limits by deployment, `-tuning` for slow networks; the normative defaults are N1.3;
4. **how `backup` obtains a consistent state of a running daemon** (N2); the requirement on the copy is a norm of N2, not a choice.
5. **the format mark and the profile `file://`**: the values `format` and `version` of its mark `FORMAT` and which versions of its format the build reads (N1.3 `format`); the on-disk format of the profile `file://`; the command by which a copy of N1.3 `copy` is taken from a store `file://` (the form of the copy is a norm) (the store pilot, A1, A2, A9).
6. **mutual exclusion of runs** of `recover`, `restore`, `import` on one installation (a lock, a mark file) — the norm requires one attempt (N3 «Races: admittance and recovery transitions»), the way is a choice (guess of stage C S02). This also declares exclusive live-daemon ownership of `-dir`, refusal of a second daemon and local operations, and release on every process termination by N1.3, and the form and place of the phase mark of `recover` (N3 «recover the store» step 1).
7. **the internal contract of `file://`** (item 5): the format of the profile `file://` is the implementation's own, not a public contract. Its description
   and the library API are written in the implementation's repository by the owner of this chapter before the code: the physical layer and the command layer — conditional
   `put`/`delete` with `guard`, the batch `batch`, CAS by version, `not_before`/`not_after`, outcomes and refusal words as at the store door (P/01.1 N1,
   N4; without retelling the norms), with vectors from its own code. Other processes of the implementation (the installer, the worker) read only
   the contract and the API; a mix of languages on `file://` is not supported — through `configstore:///` (guesses of stage C W03-E01a,
   W03-SDK-FILE-COMMAND-01, ADR-0087 addendum (12) item 7).
8. **the finite budgets of this chapter** (ADR-0130 Addendum (5) item 3, Addendum (13) items 10–12 and 14): the response-write budget, the commit wait, the number of door attempts and the replay-processing limit (R035), the limit of `admin.sock` (R008), the disk and descriptor budgets (R036). Each MUST be finite and positive; this chapter sets no value, flag or default for them. The implementation declares the value; the threshold of production acceptance is approved as in ADR-0130 Addendum (9), and a claim of the candidate alone is not enough (ADR-0130 Addendum (15) item 7).
