The canon of chapter 1.1 of lesson P/01 «Хранилище» (the Store; ADR-0080; module «Платформа» — ADR-0083). This English text
is the source of P/01.1; the Russian text [`01.1-store.norms.md`](../01.1-store.norms.md) is derived from it by translation
and is not edited by hand (ADR-0120, addendum (1)). The narrative for people is [`../01-store.md`](../../01-store.md); it is
not a norm. The door contract is [`01-store.api.openapi.yaml`](../01-store.api.openapi.yaml). How to write an implementation
from these files is [`01-store.README.md`](../01-store.README.md). Acceptance is [`../acceptance/1/`](../../acceptance/1/README.md).
Norm ids are `P/01.1-MM`; the map to former ids is [`id-map.tsv`](../../../common/id-map.tsv).

## Н0 Vocabulary

Normative keywords follow RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 (MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, MAY), as the course canon
[НОРМАТИВНЫЕ-СЛОВА.md](../../../../НОРМАТИВНЫЕ-СЛОВА.md) maps them. Requirements are written in EARS templates (When … /
If … then … / While … / The …) with the RFC keyword in place of «shall»; a plain statement of a door's answer
(«… — code and kind») is a requirement of the same force. Procedures are numbered algorithms («1. Let … 2. If …, return …»): steps
run in order, and «return» ends the procedure. Below are the common rules of М10 and М11, each in its own scope (the order
of output arrays — lessons 8–10).

### Stability of output

A common rule of the course (the Architect's decision of 2026-10-18, reconciliation of python and go М10/6–11: E19, T6, C2,
C3). When an answer is assembled from many records — rows of an event bucket, shards of a snapshot, heartbeats, reports,
rows of a family — the answer MUST NOT fail because of one unreadable record. Such a record is skipped, the answer is 200
with the rest, and the skip is counted in the field or row that the lesson names (`lines_garbled`, `garbled`,
`rows_garbled`). A 503 or a dropped connection because of one record is a violation.

- An unreadable record is one that is not JSON, not an object, not in the record form of the lesson, or not in its place.
- The exception is a record without which the answer is false; the lesson names it explicitly (for example, a marker that
  decides the outcome).
- A lesson that assembles such an answer names the skip counter and gives a step: one spoiled record — 200 and the counter.

### Time in a row

A common rule of the course for all lessons, М11 included (the Architect's decision of 2026-10-18, the store pilot). Time in
a row or object of the store, in a line of an event file, and in a JSON text inside a row is **Unix wall-clock seconds**
(from 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z) in decimal notation: the integer part is digits without leading zeros, optionally followed by a
dot and fraction digits; no sign, exponent or spaces.

- The **writer** MUST write a fraction of at most 3 digits: `^(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(\.[0-9]{1,3})?$`. A field whose form is
  `float` in [P/06.1](../../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) Н1.2 (table stamps, `sweep.at` there) is also written
  in the shortest notation.
- The **reader** MUST accept any notation of this form, with a fraction of any length: `^(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(\.[0-9]+)?$`
  (ADR-0083 item 3: a strict writer, a tolerant reader).
- In a store row, time is a string of this notation. In JSON (an object, a JCS text inside a row) the writer writes a JSON
  number of this notation unless the form of the field names a string; the reader accepts both the number and the string
  of this notation.
- A notation not of this form is a field out of form; what the reader then does is said by the rule of the field. The
  value of the notation is finite in binary64: a notation of this form whose value does not fit binary64 (overflow) is also
  out of form; the length of the fraction is still any (MAY-UNTIL-D8-01, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-25).
- The time fields of each lesson are listed at the start of its Н1 («Время»). In the data catalogue of ADR-0083 this is the
  common type «время».
- The rule does not cover numbers in inputs and answers of doors (`sent_at` of a publication, `iat` and `exp` of a token,
  `expires` of a door, `at` in `TableRow`), headers (`X-W2C-Time`), signature stamps in milliseconds, or monotonic clocks:
  their own norm sets their form. A value that a row only carries over from such an input as is (`exp` in
  `domain/revoked`, `sent_at` of a reception stamp) also follows its own norm.

### Cluster clocks

A premise of the cluster (ADR-0091 item 1): the wall clocks of its writers — the store leader, the controller, the console
door, the workers, and the writer of the event a worker writes about — differ from one another by at most `CLOCK_SKEW` =
60 s. Norms rely on it (deadlines of rows with `made` — [P/06.1](../../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) Н1.1
`platform_fields`, [P/03.6](../../../03-worker/spec/03.6-worker.norms.md), [P/04](../../../04-controller/spec/04-controller.norms.md))
but do not check it: clock synchronization is a matter of deployment, as for the domain holders
([P/08.2](../../../08-domain/spec/08.2-domain.norms.md) `rev.clocks`). A common move of all clocks backwards does not break
the premise: the order of records with time conditions is kept by the store's admission time (Н3 «Admission time»), not by
the writers' clocks.

### Order of output arrays

Scope: lessons P/08–P/10 (formerly module М11, all lessons); moved from М11/1 Н0 without a change of behaviour (review of
stage A 8–10, № 19 / decision 4).

The order of output arrays is a common rule of lessons P/08–P/10 (formerly of module М11, all its lessons; review of stage B
8–10, № 22): an array of names — members, clusters, keys, `kid`, `ref`, fingerprints, words — MUST be in ascending order of
UTF-8 bytes unless the form names another key explicitly; an array of objects — by the key named in the form. An order of
selection (for example, the keepers of [P/08.8](../../../08-domain/spec/08.8-domain.norms.md) Н1.5) is not an order of
output. Output is a view, door answers, functions of Н5, written rows and objects.

## Н1 Form

**Data** ([catalogue](../../../common/data/catalog.tsv)):
- owns: no rows — the lesson sets the store operations over any rows; the rows of the store daemon are chapter 1.3;
- reads: `/etc/w2c/configstore-rights.json` (the rights of roles to every operation, P/01.2 Н1.2), `/etc/w2c/tls/ca.pem`, `/etc/w2c/tls/<роль>/server.pem`, `/etc/w2c/tls/<роль>/server.key`, `/etc/w2c/tls/revoked`, `/etc/w2c/tls/crl.pem` (a door off the loopback, P/01.3 Н1.6).

```yaml
# A store row. The store is a map key → row; it has no other entities.
row:
  key:                       # the name of the row
    type: string
    rules:
      - not empty
      - does not start with "/"
      - no segment (between "/") equals ".."
      - at most 1024 bytes                     # at the door; a file-based implementation MAY refuse earlier (below)
    note: one spelling — one place; a key that breaks a rule gets badkey, it is not repaired
    compare: by UTF-8 bytes, without normalization — a//b, a/./b, a/b/ and the NFC and NFD forms of one text are different keys
  items:                     # the content
    type: map                # string → string; the order of fields has no meaning
    rules:
      - every key and every value is a string
      - weight (sum of byte lengths of every key and every value) at most the store's ceiling
    ceiling: 524288          # 512 KiB; the same ceiling is in the summary of P/06.1 Н1.4 (row «строка хранилища»); P/06.1 declares no other ceiling
  index:                     # the version
    type: string             # opaque: compared only for equality
    rules:
      - handed out by the store on every write; never the same twice within one store
      - 'one version per operation: a batch (Н3 «write batch») hands out one version, and every row it writes gets it; "never the same twice" counts operations, not rows'
      - '"never the same twice" is about versions handed out: a version a write or a delete hands out is not handed out again; a row carried in by import or restore keeps the copy''s version — not handed out by this store, it may equal one handed out before the transfer (P/01.3 Н1.3 import; C01-VERSION-ISSUED-01)'
      - '"" names an absent row and is no version'
    wire: an unsigned decimal number without leading zeros, up to 20 digits, as a JSON string
    note: "the wire form is a norm of the door (so the store hands out versions); opacity is a norm of the caller (it does not parse a version, it compares only for equality); a store with another version form would fit with another Н2, not with another client"

# The write condition.
cas:
  absent (no field or null): unconditional — written over whatever is there
  "":                          only while the row does not exist (creation; the first one wins)
  <version>:                   only while the version of the row is exactly this one

# A condition on another row (ADR-0074 addendum 1).
guard:
  key:   string              # the guard row: usually the writer's slot; the same key rules
  index: string              # its version, "" — the guard is absent
  note: checked in the same operation as cas; the rights to the guard row are the caller's read rights: allowed(role, read, guard.key) (P/01.2 Н1.3 guard, Н3 «проверить»)

# Admission-time conditions (ADR-0091 item 1, rev. 11) — for put and delete; admission time — Н3 «Admission time».
not_after:
  type: number               # Unix seconds; a JSON number, compared by value
  rules: [finite, at least 0]  # finite — finite in binary64, like codec time (codec.tsv time; MAY-UNTIL-D8-01): 1e400 — badrequest;
                               # value, comparison and the replay fingerprint — by the binary64 value (C01-TIME-NUMBER-EXACT-01)
  meaning: the log record applies only if its admission time is not later than not_after; otherwise expired (Н4)
  null: out of form — badrequest (as for id); a string, a bool, a negative number — also badrequest
not_before:
  type: number               # Unix seconds; a JSON number, compared by value
  rules: [finite, at least 0]
  meaning: the log record applies only if its admission time is strictly later than not_before; otherwise early (Н4)
  null: out of form — badrequest; a string, a bool, a negative number — also badrequest
  note: "both conditions MAY stand in one record; the writer writes the number in the notation of «Time in a row» Н0"

# The write identifier.
id:
  type: string
  rules: [1 to 64 lowercase hex digits]
  null: out of form — badrequest; unlike cas, null is not equal to the absence of the field
  note: "optional; a client SHOULD put it on every write (32 hex digits of randomness): after an «unknown» answer only a replay with the same id is safe (Н4; the worker — P/03.4-07: every write of it that carries a value carries an id, after ambiguous — a replay with the same id)"

# A batch (ADR-0094 B9, addendum (12) item 1): a separate operation; the single write and its guard do not change.
batch:
  op:      batch
  writes:                    # the writes; order matters; a key occurs once
    - op:    put | delete
      key:   string          # the same key rules
      items: map             # required for put, form and ceiling — row.items per row; not read for delete
      cas:   as cas of a write  # the condition of this row; no field or null — unconditional
  guards:                    # optional; no field — as []; order matters; a key occurs once, and not in writes (the condition of a written row is its cas)
    - {key, index}           # the form of guard
  id:      as id of a write  # one per batch
  not_after, not_before:     # as for a write, one condition per batch
  rules:
    - writes is not empty and has at most BATCH_WRITES elements; guards has at most BATCH_GUARDS (except the roles console and domain, below)
    - an element of writes is closed — exactly the fields op, key, items, cas; an element of guards — exactly the fields key, index
BATCH_WRITES: 64             # the limit of batch writes; exactly at the limit — passes, one more — badrequest
BATCH_GUARDS: 64             # the limit of guards; FOLDERS_DEPTH_MAX + 1 ≤ BATCH_GUARDS (P/06.8 Н1.3)
```

The role `domain` has no `BATCH_GUARDS` limit; the body weight remains: the guards of links and proofs grow with the
membership of groups ([P/08.4](../../../08-domain/spec/08.4-domain.norms.md) Н3). The limits `BATCH_WRITES` and
`BATCH_GUARDS` apply to the callers of the door, except the role `console`: the console itself keeps the limit of its batch
door ([P/06.1](../../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) Н2 «Пакетная дверь») and appends its own rows to the batch,
and its internal batches are bounded by weight and the body limit, not by the number of rows (ADR-0095 § 2, review А1).
Weight is counted over the rows of `writes`, each against the row ceiling; guards have no weight; the sum is not compared
with the ceiling, the body limit of Н2 holds it.

A file-based implementation, where a row is a file named after the key, MAY refuse a key that passes the door limit if its
encoded name does not fit a file name; it names its own limit, smaller than 1024 UTF-8 bytes, in its description (the
implementation decides).

The limit applies to the **full key** in UTF-8, including the subsystem name, the family and the separators, and acts alike
on the target key and the guard key. The limit of a single component is not the limit of the resulting path: for example,
a unit name of 1000 bytes gives `testsub/counters/…` of 1017 bytes. There is no common limit of 200 bytes for platform keys.
The admissible grammar of components does not guarantee that the full key fits the chosen store; the refusal is
`400 badkey` by Н4, with no change to the row. The application of this refusal by the unit door is
[P/06.1](../../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) Н3–Н4, by the worker — [P/03.2](../../../03-worker/spec/03.2-worker.norms.md).

## Н2 Doors

The client side — the address, the request, the answer forms for the caller, the outcomes and what to do with each, a
dropped connection and a replay — is the [store client contract](../../../common/contracts/store-client.md)
(SC-WIRE78-G01 / C08-SDK-06); here is the door side.
A process talks to the store of its server over HTTP with JSON bodies through a unix socket; on a box without a group the store MAY be opened in the process without a door — the profile `file://` «the whole box is one implementation» (P/02.4 Н1.2) — but its semantics are the same, and acceptance runs through the door. Who the caller is, is told by the role socket `/run/configstore/<роль>.sock` (P/01.2 Н1.1 `identity`).

The door contract is the file [`01-store.api.openapi.yaml`](../01-store.api.openapi.yaml) next to this text (OpenAPI 3.1; checked by loading with a tool). Routes: `GET /v1/get?key=`, `GET /v1/list?prefix=`, `POST /v1/write` (`Cmd`: put | delete; `BatchCmd`: batch; `floor` — only on the admin socket of the leader, added by P/01.3 Н2); schemas `Key`, `Row`, `Listing`, `Cmd`, `BatchCmd`, `Written`, `Fault`; codes — by Н4.

The answer forms are closed: the door MUST return only the fields of the corresponding outcome. `error` is required in a
refusal, although acceptance usually does not compare its text. A version in an answer has the form of Н1, not of an
arbitrary string.

| Answer | Required fields | Optional fields |
|---|---|---|
| a readable row | `items` — an object of strings, `index` — a non-empty version | none |
| an absent row | `items: null`, `index: ""` | none |
| a damaged row | `items: null`, `index` — its non-empty version, `unreadable: true` | none |
| a listing | `keys` — an object key → non-empty version; order by bytes | none |
| a write, a delete or a batch | `index` — the non-empty version handed out (for a batch — one for all its rows) | `again: true` only for a stored replay |
| a refusal | `kind`, `error` and the fields of its Н4 row | only `size` in a body refusal by the rule below |

The answer schemas are bound to the code and the operation: for example, a read does not return `ambiguous`, and a `409`
cannot have `kind: guard`; a batch `conflict` also carries `key` — the row whose `cas` differed, a single write has no `key`.
The size `size > limit`, the actual bytes and the order of `keys` are checked separately: one JSON Schema does not prove
these relations.

A damaged row exists and has a version: a listing returns this version, a guard compares with it, and a write and a delete
with `cas` on it pass (P/01.1-17). The version of a row is stored so that damage to the body does not lose it (how —
item 5 of «Implementation decides»); the loss of the version itself is damage to the store, not to the row: a read of this
key and a listing that touches it are 503 `unavailable` with a log entry until repaired, not an absence and not a skip
(C01-ROW-INDEX-LOST-01, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-29).

The schema `Key` sets the spelling of a key for a read, `Cmd.key` and `guard.key`. The byte limit of Н1 is checked
separately after encoding in UTF-8.
- If `Cmd.key`, `guard.key` or the read parameter `key` is absent or not a string, then the door MUST refuse with
  `badrequest` (a form violation); an empty `guard.key` is also `badrequest`.
- If a string breaks the key rules of Н1 — the target key, the read key, or a non-empty guard key — then the door MUST
  refuse with `badkey` (Н4).
- Inside `guard` the whole form is checked first — an object, exactly the fields `key` and `index`, their types, the form
  of `index`, an empty `key` — `badrequest`; and only then the key rules of the guard — `badkey` (`{key: "/x", index: "x"}`
  — `badrequest`). `guard: null` is out of form, `badrequest`.
- The elements of `writes` and `guards` of a batch are checked the same way: the `key` of a write and of a guard — form,
  then key rules; `guards: null` is out of form, `badrequest`.

`POST /v1/write` has a separate body read limit — a positive integer number of bytes named in the implementation's
description. The bytes of the HTTP body are counted, without headers, before JSON parsing; the limit is not by definition
equal to the weight ceiling of `items`.
- While the body is not larger than the limit, it passes to JSON parsing and the checks of Н3.
- If the body exceeds the limit, then the door MUST refuse with `413 toolarge` before calling the store operation: the row,
  the version and the `id` memory do not change.
- `limit` in this refusal is the body limit; when the row weight is exceeded — the row ceiling.
- If the full size of the body is known, the body refusal MAY contain `size` with this size in bytes; if it is unknown,
  `size` is absent. `key` is not returned in a body refusal: the JSON has not been parsed yet.
- The acceptance settings are given in Н5; the way to set the limit is the implementation's choice.

Extra top-level fields of the command `POST /v1/write` (outside `op`, `key`, `items`, `cas`, `guard`, `not_after`,
`not_before`, `id`) are accepted and not read regardless of their JSON type; they do not affect the write or the replay
fingerprint. Their bytes count towards the total size of the HTTP body. This rule does not extend to the content of
`guard`: its form is closed by Н1; `items` is checked by Н1 for put and not read for delete by Н3.
For `op: batch` the extra top-level fields are those outside `op`, `writes`, `guards`, `id`, `not_after`, `not_before`;
among them are `key`, `items`, `cas`, `guard` of a single write: they are accepted and not read. The elements of `writes`
and `guards` are closed (Н1).

If a route does not exist, then the door MUST answer `404 {kind: badrequest}`. The list of codes and kinds is closed: a new
one only by an edit of this lesson.

### Н2.1 Request parsing

Parsing is the same in all implementations: on one input they MUST give one code and one refusal kind.

- **Query.** The string after `?` is parsed as `application/x-www-form-urlencoded`
  ([WHATWG URL, urlencoded parsing](https://url.spec.whatwg.org/#urlencoded-parsing)): pairs separated by `&`, name and value
  by the first `=`, `+` is a space, `%XX` is a byte; a `%` not followed by two hex digits stays as written.
  The whole string is parsed by this algorithm: empty pairs (`&&`) are skipped, a parameter without `=` has the value `""` —
  `?key` gives an empty key, `badkey` (Н1). A client sends a `+` of a key as `%2B`, a `%` as `%25`.
- Order of query checks: form first (repetition, absence of the parameter) — `badrequest`, then UTF-8 and the key rules of
  Н1 — `badkey`.
- If `key` of a read or `prefix` of a listing is repeated, then the door MUST answer `400 badrequest`: the request is out of
  form. An absent `key` is `400 badrequest` (above); an absent `prefix` is an empty prefix, all rows.
- If the bytes of `key` after decoding are not UTF-8, then the door MUST answer `400 badkey`: a key is a string of Н1.
  `prefix` is bytes after decoding, UTF-8 is not required (Н3 «list»).
- Other query parameters, a query of `POST /v1/write` and a body of `GET` are not read; `Content-Type` is not checked.
- **The body of `POST /v1/write`** is JSON by [RFC 8259](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8259): a repeated field name
  in any object of the body (§4), `NaN`, `Infinity`, a BOM and non-UTF-8 bytes (§8.1) — `400 badrequest`. A lone surrogate
  (`\ud800`) in `key` or `guard.key`, or for a batch in the `key` of an element of `writes` or `guards` — `400 badkey`: the
  string cannot be encoded in UTF-8 nor measured by Н1; in a name or value of `items` — `400 badrequest`; in extra fields
  it is not read. Numbers and strings of extra fields are not read at any length. The nesting depth is one number for all
  implementations: `JSON_DEPTH` = 128 nested objects and arrays from the root, the root object counts 1, in any field,
  extra ones too; 129 — `400 badrequest`.
- A body in `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` is measured by the decoded bytes; exceeding the limit is `413 toolarge` without
  `size`.
- **Early refusal.** When a door refuses before reading the whole body (`Content-Length` larger than the limit — `413`;
  any other refusal before the body), the door MUST send the full answer with `Connection: close`, then read and discard
  what was sent, without parsing, up to the declared length but no more than the drain volume and no longer than the drain
  time, and only then close the connection: a client that is still sending the body gets the answer, not a connection
  reset. The rule is one for all HTTP doors of the module (console —
  [P/06.5](../../../06-console/spec/06.5-console.norms.md) Н2 «Разбор запроса консоли», the byte door — P/06.1 Н2, the
  resource door — P/05 Н2 «Разбор запроса»); the drain volume and time are item 9 of «Implementation decides».
- **The path** is compared with the routes of Н2 byte for byte, without percent-decoding and normalization: `/v1/%67et`,
  `/v1/get/`, `//v1/get` and a target in absolute form (`http://host/v1/get`) are not routes of Н2, `404 badrequest`.
- **Method and HTTP.** A method or a path outside Н2 is `404 {kind: badrequest}` with a `Fault` body, an unknown method on a
  known path (`PATCH /v1/get`) included; `HEAD` is also `404`, without a body (RFC 9110 §9.3.2). `Host` is not checked: the
  door is a unix socket, and an absent, repeated or any value of `Host` is no refusal.
- **Request line and headers.** A line ends only with CRLF: a lone LF or CR — `400 badrequest`. Empty lines before the
  request line — `400 badrequest` (they are not skipped). The request target is only the bytes 0x21–0x7E without `#`; any
  other byte (a raw `\x00` included) — `400 badrequest`; `%00` is three admissible bytes: the path is compared byte for
  byte, and the query key after percent-decoding contains U+0000 and is admissible by Н1. The header limit is
  `HEADER_BYTES` = 65536: the sum of the lengths of the field lines without their CRLF and without the final empty line;
  exactly at the limit — parsed, one byte more — `400 badrequest`; there is no separate limit on the number of lines.
- **Body framing** follows RFC 9112 §6–7: `Content-Length` is digits only (`+5`, a space inside — `400`); several
  `Content-Length` with different values — `400` and the connection closed; a length number larger than the
  implementation can represent — `413 toolarge` (it is certainly larger than the body limit, no `size`); a chunk size of
  chunked is HEXDIG only (`0x5` — `400`); a `Transfer-Encoding` other than exactly `chunked` — `400` (`gzip, chunked` and
  two TE lines too). A truncated body — EOF before `Content-Length` or no last chunk of chunked — is not executed: no
  answer, no write, the `id` memory does not change. After any framing error the door MUST close the connection. The
  request line, the headers and the body framing are parsed by [RFC 9112](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9112): obs-fold
  and a space before the colon of a header are parsing violations (§5.1–5.2); with `Content-Length` and
  `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` together the body is read as chunked (§6.3). A request that does not parse as HTTP/1.1 or
  HTTP/1.0 (no version, a wrong request line or header, a request line or headers longer than the limit of item 9) is
  `400 {kind: badrequest}` with a `Fault` body. The codes 414, 431, 501, 505 and default server pages are not in the
  closed list.
- **Order of command checks** when there are several violations: the body is a JSON object → `op` → the socket for `floor`
  (`op: floor` not on the admin socket — `403 forbidden`, P/01.3 Н4) → `key` (form — `badrequest`, rules of Н1 — `badkey`) →
  `items` for put → `cas` → `guard` (form — `badrequest`, then key rules — `badkey`) → `id` → `not_after` → `not_before`
  (form — `badrequest`; ADR-0091 item 1) → weight → rights. The refusal kind is that of the first violation.
- **Order of batch checks:** the body is a JSON object → `op` → `writes` (present, an array, not empty, at most
  `BATCH_WRITES`) → its elements in order: an object with a closed set of fields → `op` → `key` (form — `badrequest`, rules
  of Н1 — `badkey`) → `items` for put → `cas` → `guards` (an array, at most `BATCH_GUARDS`) → its elements in order (form —
  `badrequest`, then key rules — `badkey`) → repeated keys: twice in `writes`, twice in `guards`, a guard on a key from
  `writes` — `badrequest` → `id` → `not_after` → `not_before` → weight → rights. The refusal kind is that of the first
  violation.

## Н3 Procedures

The vocabulary in which all procedures of the course are written. An operation is one indivisible action of the store:
nobody comes between its steps; whatever comes, comes before or after. In a group of servers «indivisible» means «one log
record applied by every server alike»; on one box — «under one lock» (the implementation decides, see below). A batch is,
moreover, indivisible against a power loss: «write batch», «Atomicity».

### read(key) → (items, index)

1. If the key fails the checks of Н1, return `badkey`.
2. If the caller has no read right, return `forbidden` (P/01.2 Н3 «проверить»: `allowed(роль, read, key)`).
3. If the row `key` does not exist, return (null, "").
4. If the row exists but is not readable (Н2), return (null, index, `unreadable: true`).
5. Return (a copy of items, index).

The read MUST be linearizable: it returns a state not older than the last completed write seen by any caller. In a group this means that a read goes through the leader of the current term (a lease renewal is a read, and a follower cut off from the group must not confirm an epoch that the others have already moved; [P/03.2 Н3 «продлить()» and «подтвердить(id)»](../../../03-worker/spec/03.2-worker.norms.md)). The mechanism — the implementation decides.

**Races.** A read between two writes sees either the first or the second, never a half. There are no others.

### list(prefix) → {key: index}

1. For each row whose key starts with prefix (byte comparison), output its index.
2. Order the keys by bytes.

`prefix` is any byte string, not a key: the key rules of Н1 do not apply to it, the segment `..` means nothing, an empty prefix is all rows; the prefix check gives no 400 except for a repeated parameter (Н2.1) and does not check the read right; a listing returns all rows under the prefix, including those outside the role's read rights. Identity is checked before the operation by P/01.2 Н3 «проверить»: a substituted role or an untrusted forwarding gives 403 forbidden. Н2 of the door: 200, 403 of identity, 404 no route, 503. A listing does not require read rights to values: it names names and versions, not content (ADR-0021). A prefix SHOULD end with `/`: `testsub/count` would match both `testsub/counters/…` and `testsub/countdown`.

**Races.** A write during a listing: the row is either in the list with its version or not; a listing does not block a write. There are no others.

### Admission time

Every log record (put, delete, batch, the service `floor` of P/01.3) gets an **admission time** — Unix seconds (ADR-0091 item 1):

```
admitted := max(the leader's clock when the record is admitted to the log, the admission time of the previous log record)
```

The leader assigns it when admitting the record to the log and stores it in the log record itself. When a record is
**applied**, the conditions `not_after` and `not_before` MUST be checked against this stored time, not against the clock of
the applying server: the outcome is one on all servers of the group and does not depend on when the record reached
application. The admission time MUST NOT decrease along the log: a new leader and a restarted daemon continue from the
admission time of the last record of their log (including one that came by snapshot). A store without a log — a box
(`box` P/01.3 Н1.3) and a store in the process — assigns the time by the same rule from its last admission time and MUST
store it as durably as rows: under the same lock and the same durability barrier as the write (item 3 of «Implementation
decides»). The admission time is part of the store state and its snapshot (P/01.3 Н1.3 `admission`); the copy `copy` of
P/01.3 does not carry it: a group after `import` or `restore` starts from the clock of its leader. A write answer does not
return it. A leader clock that ran ahead raises the admission time, and writes with `not_after` get `expired` until the
clock catches up; this is visible by the flag `admission_ahead` (P/01.3 Н2 `/v1/status`).

**Races.** A delete with `not_before` = T and a write with `not_after` = T of one row in any order of arrival: the delete is
admitted only at an admission time > T; every log record after it has an admission time not smaller, that is > T —
`expired`; a write admitted before the delete stands earlier in the log and is removed by the delete (its CAS on the
version of the delete will not pass). A common move of wall clocks backwards does not change the order: the admission time
does not decrease.

### write(key, items, cas?, guard?, not_after?, not_before?, id?) → index

1. If the key, or the form of cas, guard, id, not_after or not_before, fails the checks of Н1, return `badkey` / `badrequest`.
2. If the weight of items exceeds the ceiling, return `toolarge` (nothing written).
3. If the caller has no write right to key, return `forbidden` (P/01.2 Н3 «проверить»: `allowed(роль, write, key)`).
4. If guard is given and the caller has no read right to guard.key, return `forbidden`.
5. The remaining steps run as one operation, when the log record is applied.
6. Let admitted be the admission time of this record (above, «Admission time»).
7. If id is given and has already been applied:
   1. If that record was different (op, key, items, cas, guard, not_after, not_before), return `badrequest`.
   2. Otherwise return its answer with again=true; the state does not change.
8. If not_after is given and admitted > not_after, return `expired`; nothing is written.
9. If not_before is given and admitted ≤ not_before, return `early`; nothing is written.
10. Let current be the version of the row key ("" if absent).
11. If guard is given:
    1. Let at be the version of the row guard.key ("" if absent).
    2. If at ≠ guard.index, return `guard` {key: guard.key, index: at}; nothing is written.
12. If cas is given and cas ≠ current, return `conflict` {index: current}; nothing is written.
13. Let index be a new version, never handed out before by this store; the order of versions is opaque.
14. Set the row key to (a copy of items, index).
15. If id is given, remember (id → this record, the answer).
16. Return index.

The order of checks inside the operation is normative: the `id` memory, then `not_after` and `not_before`, then the guard,
then CAS; with several violations the answer is by the first one (ADR-0091 item 1). A replay of an applied write gets its
answer also after `not_after` — including a replay that arrived while the first copy was not yet applied: the first one
is applied first, the replay answers with its answer. A refusal `expired` and `early`, like `conflict` and `guard`, makes no write and does not take the `id`, but the admission time of the refused
record remains the last admission time (in a group — a log record; a box and a store in the process store it as durably
as rows; C01-ADMITTED-ON-REFUSAL-01): the same
write with the same `id` will get the same refusal (the admission time does not decrease), and the caller repeats `early` with a new write at
the next step (Н4). The guard before CAS. A guard refusal means a mismatch of the observed version and the absence of this write. The caller re-reads the condition and decides anew whether it has the right to repeat; a version mismatch by itself does not prove a loss of ownership. A CAS refusal also requires a re-read before a new attempt. A guard on an absent row (`index: ""`) holds while the row does not exist.

A version is handed out only to a completed write: a refusal does not spend a version. Items are copied: the store does not share memory with the caller, neither on input nor on output.

The «different record» of a replay is compared by the values of the fingerprint fields, not by the bytes of the body:
`cas: null` equals the absence of `cas`, the order of the fields of `items` and the way JSON strings are escaped have no
meaning; `not_after` and `not_before` — by the value of the number (`10` and `10.0` are one), absence equals no number.

**Races.**
- Two writers with one `cas` on one row: exactly one passes, the second gets `conflict` with the version of the first.
- Two creations with `cas: ""`: exactly one passes.
- A write under a guard and a change of the guard (a new capture of the slot) in any order: either the write is made before the change, or it is refused with `guard` and the new version of the guard; there is no state «written after the change».
- A replay of a write with the same `id` after an «unknown» answer: if the first one applied, the replay answers with its answer and changes nothing; if not, it applies as the first. The caller does not distinguish these cases and must not.
- A replay with the same `id` but other content (other `items`, `cas` or `guard`): refusal `badrequest`, nothing written — an identifier belongs to one write.
- A leader change between admission and application: the caller gets `ambiguous`; see above.
- A write with `not_after` and its replay with the same `id` after `not_after`: if the first one applied, the replay answers
  with its answer; if not, the replay applies as the first and gets `expired` (ADR-0091 item 1).

### delete(key, cas?, guard?, not_after?, not_before?, id?) → index

The field `items`, if sent, is not read regardless of its JSON type; its presence is not a refusal.

1. If the key, or the form of cas, guard, id, not_after or not_before, fails the checks of Н1, return `badkey` / `badrequest`.
2. If the caller has no delete right to key, return `forbidden` (P/01.2 Н3 «проверить»: never Н1.3, `allowed(роль, delete, key)`).
3. If guard is given and the caller has no read right to guard.key, return `forbidden`.
4. The remaining steps run as one operation, when the log record is applied.
5. Let admitted be the admission time of this record.
6. Replay by id: compare (op, key, cas, guard, not_after, not_before); items is not read and is not part of the
   fingerprint, even if sent with another JSON type. If they match, return the former answer with again=true; otherwise
   return `badrequest`.
7. If not_after is given and admitted > not_after, return `expired`.
8. If not_before is given and admitted ≤ not_before, return `early`.
9. Let current be the version of the row key ("" if absent).
10. If guard is given and the version of guard.key ≠ guard.index, return `guard`.
11. If cas is given and cas ≠ current, return `conflict` {index: current}.
12. Remove the row key (an absent row is no error).
13. Let index be a new version (a delete spends a version).
14. If id is given, remember.
15. Return index.

A delete spends a version so that a stale `cas` on a deleted and re-created row does not pass. A delete of an absent row is done: a replay of a done delete is the same delete.

**Races.** As for a write. A delete and a write of one row with one `cas` on an **existing** version: one passes — the delete spends the version. With `cas ""` on an absent row a delete changes nothing (the row was absent and stays absent; the read version stays `""`), and a following write with `cas ""` passes — there is nothing to choose a winner from here.

### write batch(writes, guards?, not_after?, not_before?, id?) → index

1. If the form of the batch fails the checks of Н1 (order — Н2.1), return `badkey` / `badrequest`.
2. For each put in the order of writes: if the weight of its items exceeds the ceiling, return `toolarge` {key, size, limit} of this row; nothing is written.
3. Rights in the order of writes: `allowed(роль, write | delete, key)`; then in the order of guards: `allowed(роль, read, key)`;
   if one is missing, return `forbidden` for the whole batch (P/01.2 Н3 «проверить»); nothing is written.
4. The remaining steps run as one operation, when the log record is applied.
5. Let admitted be the admission time of this record (above, «Admission time»).
6. If id is given and has already been applied:
   1. If that record was different (fingerprint below), return `badrequest`.
   2. Otherwise return its answer with again=true; the state does not change.
7. If not_after is given and admitted > not_after, return `expired`; nothing is written.
8. If not_before is given and admitted ≤ not_before, return `early`; nothing is written.
9. For each guard in the order of guards: let at be the version of the row key ("" if absent); if at ≠ the index of the
   guard, return `guard` {key, index: at}; nothing is written.
10. For each write in the order of writes: let current be the version of the row key ("" if absent); if cas is given and
    cas ≠ current, return `conflict` {key, index: current}; nothing is written.
11. Let index be a new version, never handed out before by this store — one per batch.
12. For each write in the order of writes: put → set the row key to (a copy of items, index); delete → remove the row key
    (an absent row is no error).
13. If id is given, remember (id → this batch, the answer).
14. Return index.

The order is normative (ADR-0094 addendum (12) item 1): form → weight → rights → the `id` memory → deadlines → guards in
list order → `cas` in list order → application; the answer is the first violation. A guard on a written key is a form
refusal: the condition of such a row is its `cas`.

The refusals `expired`, `early`, `guard`, `conflict` make no write, do not take the `id` and do not spend versions; the
admission time of a refused batch remains the last admission time, as for a write. A refusal names the row but not the
value: `412 guard {key, index}`, `409 conflict {key, index}` — the version of this row now.

One version per operation: all put rows get the `index` of the batch; a delete spends the same version. A delete of an absent row is done.

**Replay fingerprint** — `writes` in order (`op`, `key`, `cas`, for put — `items`), the set of `guards`, `not_after`,
`not_before`. Values are compared as for a write: `cas: null` equals absence, the order of the fields of `items` has no
meaning; the order of the elements of `guards` has no meaning, the absence of `guards` equals `[]`; the order of the
elements of `writes` matters; `items` of a delete is not read and not included. The `id` memory is one for writes and
batches, its lifetime — the section below, a batch counts as one write: an `id` taken by a put is `badrequest` for a
batch, and vice versa.

**Atomicity** (ADR-0094 addendum (12) item 2). A batch MUST become visible as a whole or not at all: a read and a listing
see all its rows or none. After a power loss at any point, a barrier refusal included, the whole batch or none of its rows
is visible; the `id` memory is durable together with it: a replay does not complete half a batch, because there is no
half. The `ambiguous` branch of Н4 (a medium error, a barrier refusal, a leader change) applies to the whole batch: a
replay with the same `id` answers with the first answer or applies the batch as the first. In a group a batch is one log
record; the way of a box and of a store in the process is item 3 of «Implementation decides».

**Races.**
- Two batches with a common row and one `cas`: exactly one passes, the second gets `conflict` with the version of the first; none of its rows is written.
- A batch under a guard and a change of the guard in any order: either the batch is made before the change, or it is refused with `guard` and the new version; there is no state «part written after the change».
- Two reads of two rows of a batch are two operations: the first may be before the batch, the second after. One operation sees the batch whole; the caller compares versions: the rows of one batch have one version.
- A replay with the same `id` together with the first application: as for a write, the first copy is applied first.

### Replay by identifier — store memory

The store MUST answer a replay with the same answer until both conditions hold: more than two minutes have passed since the first application **and** at least 50 000 records with an identifier have been applied after it; when both hold, it MAY forget. A forgotten replay is applied as a new write — hence `cas` on every write that matters.

**Races.** Two replays of one `id` at once: one is applied (under the lock or in the log), the second gets the same answer with `again: true`; a replay and a new write with the same `id` but other content — the earlier one is applied first, the second gets 400.

## Implementation decides

Each implementation names in its description:
1. **the indivisibility mechanism** on one box (a file lock, a mutex) and in a group (a consensus log; a batch is one log record) and the lock wait time — what the caller gets when it expires (as `unavailable`);
2. **how a version is handed out**: a counter in a file, a log index plus the group base — as long as it does not repeat; which number it starts from;
3. **the disk layout** and **the durability barriers**. The order of one write: bytes to the medium, a barrier, publication and the answer; a delete survives a power loss. If the barrier refused when the bytes are already on the medium, the row is published and the answer is `ambiguous` (Н4); a replay with the same `id` repeats the barrier. A mode without barriers is admissible only by a flag of the «Стенд» command of acceptance; there is no environment variable or operational flag that turns barriers off (P/01.3 Н1.3 `durability`; the store pilot, А10). **A batch:** how it becomes visible as a whole and after a power loss stays whole or does not appear at all (Н3 «write batch», «Atomicity») — an intent log with a barrier before the rows are published, or another way; a lock is not enough: a lock does not survive power, and the rows of different files reach the medium separately;
4. **the key length limit** of a file-based implementation, if it is smaller than 1024;
5. **how a store that can read its row only partially** (a damaged file) reports it: the answer form is a norm (Н2 `Row.unreadable`, P/01.1-17); here — how the implementation detects the damage and what it logs, and how it stores the version of the row so that damage to the body does not lose it (the loss of the version — 503, Н2; C01-ROW-INDEX-LOST-01, the Architect's decision of 2026-10-29);
6. **the lifetime and volume of the replay memory** beyond the minimum of Н3;
7. **how the door tells «not added to the log» from «unknown»**; the leader wait time before `unavailable` is 5 s from the loss of the leader, a norm of P/01.3 Н1.3, not a choice.
8. **the read limit of the HTTP body** of a store in the process without a daemon; for the daemon it is set by the flag `-max-body` (P/01.3 Н1.3 «Флаги демона», default 1 MiB) separately from the row ceiling `-max-value`; the value and meaning of `limit` — by Н2, the acceptance parameters — by Н5 (log of cammeta-python, lesson 11 № 24).
9. **the request parsing limits**: the length of the HTTP request line (in bytes, without the final CRLF); exceeding it is `400 badrequest` by Н2.1; the length of a chunk-size line of chunked with extensions (without CRLF) — exceeding it is a framing error: `400 badrequest` and the connection closed (C01-CHUNK-LINE-LIMIT-01); the drain volume and time after an early refusal (Н2.1). The header limit (`HEADER_BYTES` 65536) and the JSON depth (`JSON_DEPTH` 128) are norms of Н2.1, not a choice (reconciliation of python and go 2026-10-18);
10. **how the runner obtains an isolated instance** with the acceptance parameters of Н5: the start command with substitutions by the section «Стенд» of the acceptance README of this lesson — it is common to the acceptance of М10; the lesson does not norm an internal API;
11. **how a listing takes its list** (a copy under the lock, a version snapshot); the guarantee of the «Races» of Н3 is kept.
