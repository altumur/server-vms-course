## N0 Vocabulary

<!-- req: P/01.1-R001 -->
Normative keywords follow RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 (MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, MAY), as the course canon [NORMATIVE-TERMS.md](../../common/NORMATIVE-TERMS.md) maps them. Requirements are written in EARS templates (When … / If … then … / While … / The …) with the RFC keyword in place of «shall»; a plain statement of a door's answer («… — code and kind») is a requirement of the same force. Below are the common rules of M10 and M11, each in its own scope. Procedures are numbered algorithms («1. Let … 2. If …, return …»): steps run in order, and «return» ends the procedure.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R301 -->
The annotation «(judge-only: …)» identifies a specific file, row or case outside the implementation input M. All implementation duties are stated in the visible norms.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R302 -->
A requirement whose paragraph starts with the label «**Scope: production.**» (a checklist item — right after its id) belongs only to production qualification ([production acceptance](../qualification/README.md) (judge-only: qualification procedures)): functional gates do not establish production qualification, which that contract judges separately from the functional scenarios; a missing input, budget or binding there is `blocked`/`NotExecuted`, not a successful production result. A requirement without the label binds every implementation, also when only production acceptance or reading checks it.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R151 -->
Marks of the readiness checklist. Right after its id (after the label «**Scope: production.**» when the item has it), a checklist item names in square brackets how it is closed: `test` — by an execution of the implementation: its own test (the norm trace of the generation cycle) and/or a scenario, a seam or a vector of acceptance; `reading` — only by reading the code or the description of the implementation, a test does not close it; `stand` — only by a stand outside the own tests of the implementation: production acceptance (`RUN-*`) and the probes of a real stand (`probes/README.md`). A probe of the probe debt table is not executed yet: until it is, its part is `reading`. The store group stand is not `stand`: a test of the implementation raises the group too, and such an item is `test`. Several words stand comma-separated, `[test, reading]`; each word names the part of the item it closes.

### Stability of output

<!-- req: P/01.1-R002 -->
A common rule of the course. When an answer is assembled from many records — rows of an event bucket, shards of a snapshot, heartbeats, reports, rows of a family — the answer MUST NOT fail because of one unreadable record. Such a record is skipped, the answer is 200 with the rest, and the skip is counted in the field or row that the lesson names (`lines_garbled`, `garbled`, `rows_garbled`). A 503 or a dropped connection because of one record is a violation. An unreadable record is one that is not JSON, not an object, not in the record form of the lesson, or not in its place. The exception is a record without which the answer is false; the lesson names it explicitly (for example, a marker that decides the outcome). A lesson that assembles such an answer names the skip counter and gives a step: one spoiled record — 200 and the counter.

### Time in a row

<!-- req: P/01.1-R006 -->
A common rule of the course for all lessons, M11 included. Time in a row or object of the store, in a line of an event file, and in a JSON text inside a row is **Unix wall-clock seconds** (from 1970-01-01T00:00:00Z) in decimal notation: the integer part is digits without leading zeros, optionally followed by a dot and fraction digits; no sign, exponent or spaces.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R007 -->
The **writer** MUST write a fraction of at most 3 digits: `^(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(\.[0-9]{1,3})?$`. A field whose form is `float` in [P/06.1](../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) N1.2 (table stamps, `sweep.at` there) is also written by the writer in the shortest notation.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R304 -->
Private admission metadata is not a row time field subject to this writer notation. It uses the finite binary64 numeric domain of N3 «Admission time» and MUST preserve its assigned value through storage and restoration, including fractions longer than this row-writer notation permits. This exception prescribes no internal encoding or additional wire field.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R008 -->
The **reader** MUST accept the same form with at most three fractional digits as the current writer: `^(0|[1-9][0-9]*)(\.[0-9]{1,3})?$`. Excess precision has the existing field/row unreadable outcome of that field, without rounding or truncation.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R009 -->
In a store row, time is a string of this notation.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R305 -->
In JSON (an object, a JCS text inside a row) the writer writes a JSON number of this notation unless the form of the field names a string.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R306 -->
In JSON the reader accepts both the number and the string of this notation.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R010 -->
A notation outside this form is a field out of form; the field rule determines the reader outcome. The reader has the writer's fraction bound. The R304 exception and own forms of R012 are not narrowed by this rule. The value of a time notation is finite in binary64: overflow is also out of form.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R012 -->
The time fields of each lesson are listed at the start of its N1 («Time»). In the data catalogue this is the common type «time». The rule does not cover numbers in inputs and answers of doors (`sent_at` of a publication, `iat` and `exp` of a token, `expires` of a door, `at` in `TableRow`), headers (`X-W2C-Time`), signature stamps in milliseconds, or monotonic clocks: their own norm sets their form. A value that a row only carries over from such an input as is (`exp` in `domain/revoked`, `sent_at` of a reception stamp) also follows its own norm.

### Cluster clocks

<!-- req: P/01.1-R013 -->
A premise of the cluster: the wall clocks of its writers — the store leader, the controller, the console door, the workers, and the writer of the event a worker writes about — differ from one another by at most `CLOCK_SKEW` from [forms/runtime-constants.tsv](../forms/runtime-constants.tsv). Norms rely on it (deadlines of rows with `made` — [P/06.1](../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) N1.1 `platform_fields`, [P/03.6](../../03-worker/spec/03.6-worker.norms.md), [P/04](../../04-controller/spec/04-controller.norms.md)) but do not check it: clock synchronization is a matter of deployment, as for the domain holders ([P/08.2](../../08-domain/spec/08.2-domain.norms.md) `rev.clocks`). A common move of all clocks backwards does not break the premise: the order of records with time conditions is kept by the store's admission time (N3 «Admission time»), not by the writers' clocks. The premise of deployment is broken by the actual spread between the clocks beyond `CLOCK_SKEW`, not by the amplitude of a common jump: a common shift and a return are allowed. At a common move of the clocks the consequences follow their own conditions: admission time does not decrease (P/01.1-R070); `expired` depends on `not_after` (P/01.1-R268); `admission_ahead` appears when the current clock of the leader is behind the saved admission time (P/01.1-R265; N3 «Admission time», P/01.3 N1.3 `admission`). No new route is added for it.

## N1 Form

<!-- req: P/01.1-R016 -->
**Data** ([catalogue](../../common/data/catalog.tsv)):
- owns: no rows — the lesson sets the store operations over any rows; the rows of the store daemon are chapter 1.3;
- reads: `/etc/w2c/configstore-rights.json` (the rights of roles to every operation, P/01.2 N1.2), `/etc/w2c/tls/ca.pem`, `/etc/w2c/tls/<role>/server.pem`, `/etc/w2c/tls/<role>/server.key`, `/etc/w2c/tls/revoked`, `/etc/w2c/tls/crl.pem` (a door off the loopback, P/01.3 N1.6).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R019 -->
```yaml
# A store row.
row:
  key:                       # the name of the row
    type: string
  items:                     # the content
    type: map
    ceiling: ROW_CEILING
  index:                     # the version
    type: string
    wire: "^(0|[1-9][0-9]{0,19})$"

# The write condition.
cas: [absent, "", <version>]

# A condition on another row.
guard:
  key:   string              # the guard row, usually the writer's slot; as row.key
  index: string              # its version

# Admission-time conditions.
not_after:
  type: number               # Unix seconds
not_before:
  type: number               # Unix seconds

# The write identifier.
id:
  type: string
  pattern: "^[0-9a-f]{1,64}$"

# A batch.
batch:
  op:      batch
  writes:
    - op:    put | delete
      key:   string          # as row.key
      items: map             # as row.items
      cas:   as cas of a write
  guards:
    - {key, index}           # as guard
  id:      as id of a write
  not_after, not_before:     # as for a write
BATCH_WRITES: forms/runtime-constants.tsv             # the limit of batch writes
BATCH_GUARDS: forms/runtime-constants.tsv             # the limit of guards
```

The store is a map key → row; it has no other entities.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R156 -->
A key MUST NOT be empty. A key MUST NOT start with `/`. A key MUST NOT have a segment (between `/`) equal to `..`. At the door a key MUST be at most `KEY_BYTES` bytes in every implementation. If a key breaks a key rule, then the store MUST answer `badkey`; the key is not repaired. One spelling of a key is one place: keys are compared by UTF-8 bytes, without normalization — `a//b`, `a/./b`, `a/b/` and the NFC and NFD forms of one text are different keys.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R159 -->
Every key and every value of `items` MUST be a string. The order of the fields of `items` has no meaning. `items` MUST keep its weight (sum of byte lengths of every key and every value) at most the store's ceiling `ROW_CEILING`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R162 -->
The store MUST hand out a version on every write. A version is never the same twice within one store. One version per operation: a batch (N3 «write batch») hands out one version, and every row it writes gets it; «never the same twice» counts operations, not rows. «Never the same twice» is about versions handed out: a version a write or a delete hands out is not handed out again; a row carried in by import or restore keeps the copy's version (P/01.3-R140) — not handed out by this store, it may equal one handed out before the transfer (P/01.3 N1.3 import). `""` names an absent row and is no version.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R166 -->
A version is opaque, and opacity is a norm of the caller: the caller does not parse a version, it compares versions only for equality.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R167 -->
The wire form of a version (`wire`) is a norm of the door: so the store hands out versions. A store with another version form would fit with another N2, not with another client.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R168 -->
If `cas` is absent (no field or `null`), then the write is unconditional: it is written over whatever is there. If `cas` is `""`, then the write MUST apply only while the row does not exist (creation; the first one wins). If `cas` is a version, then the write MUST apply only while the version of the row is exactly this one.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R171 -->
`guard.index` is the version of the guard row; `""` means the guard row is absent. The guard MUST be checked in the same operation as `cas`. The rights to the guard row are the caller's read rights: `allowed(role, read, guard.key)` (P/01.2 N1.3 guard, N3 «check»).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R174 -->
The admission-time conditions `not_after` and `not_before` are for put and delete; admission time is N3 «Admission time». `not_after` and `not_before` MUST be finite and at least 0; finite is finite in binary64, like codec time (codec.tsv `time`): `1e400` is `badrequest`. `not_after` and `not_before` are JSON numbers compared by value: the value and the comparison go by the binary64 value; the replay fingerprint goes by P/01.1-R090. If `not_after` or `not_before` is `null`, a string, a bool or a negative number, then the door MUST refuse with `badrequest`: it is out of form (as for `id`).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R178 -->
A log record with `not_after` MUST apply only if its admission time is not later than `not_after`; otherwise the answer is `expired` (N4). A log record with `not_before` MUST apply only if its admission time is strictly later than `not_before`; otherwise the answer is `early` (N4). Both conditions MAY stand in one record.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R181 -->
The writer writes the number of `not_after` and `not_before` in the notation of «Time in a row» N0.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R182 -->
If `id` is `null`, then the door MUST refuse with `badrequest`: it is out of form; unlike `cas`, `null` is not equal to the absence of the field.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R183 -->
`id` is optional. A client SHOULD put `id` on every write (32 hex digits of randomness). After an «unknown» answer only a replay with the same `id` is safe (N4; the worker — P/03.4-07: every write of it that carries a value carries an id, after `ambiguous` — a replay with the same id).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R185 -->
A batch is a separate operation; the single write and its guard do not change. The order of `writes` matters. A key occurs in `writes` once. `items` of an element of `writes` is required for put. For put, the form of `items` of an element of `writes` is that of `row.items`; its ceiling is that of `row.items`, per row (R200). `items` of an element of `writes` is not read for delete.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R189 -->
`guards` is optional; no field is as `[]`. The order of `guards` matters. A key occurs in `guards` once. A key of `guards` does not occur in `writes`: the condition of a written row is its `cas`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R192 -->
A batch has one `id` and one condition `not_after`, `not_before` for the whole batch, as for a write.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R193 -->
`writes` MUST NOT be empty and MUST have at most `BATCH_WRITES` elements. `guards` MUST have at most `BATCH_GUARDS` elements (except the roles `console` and `domain`, R020, R315).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R194 -->
An element of `writes` is closed — exactly the fields `op`, `key`, `items`, `cas`. An element of `guards` is closed — exactly the fields `key`, `index`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R195 -->
Exactly at the limit `BATCH_WRITES` the batch passes; one more is `badrequest`. `BATCH_GUARDS` is at least `FOLDERS_DEPTH_MAX + 1` (P/06.8 N1.3). `KEY_BYTES`, `BATCH_WRITES` and `BATCH_GUARDS` are the named limits in [runtime-constants.tsv](../forms/runtime-constants.tsv).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R198 -->
`ROW_CEILING` is the configured ceiling of this store instance; on a daemon it is the effective `-max-value`, whose default is owned by [daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R199 -->
An in-process store uses its declared ceiling and the N5 stand parameter without a required flag or internal API. This is not a second fixed row ceiling.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R020 -->
The role `domain` has no `BATCH_GUARDS` limit: the guards of links and proofs grow with the membership of groups ([P/08.4](../../08-domain/spec/08.4-domain.norms.md) N3). The body weight limit still applies to the role `domain`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R315 -->
The limits `BATCH_WRITES` and `BATCH_GUARDS` apply to the callers of the door, except the role `console`: the console itself keeps the limit of its batch door ([P/06.1](../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) N2 «Batch door») and appends its own rows to the batch, and its internal batches are bounded by weight and the body limit, not by the number of rows.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R200 -->
Weight is counted over the rows of `writes`, each against the row ceiling. Guards have no weight. The sum of the weights of the rows of `writes` is not compared with the ceiling: the body limit of N2 holds it.

<!-- removed: P/01.1-R021 by ADR-0133 -->

<!-- req: P/01.1-R022 -->
The limit applies to the **full key** in UTF-8, including the subsystem name, the family and the separators; it bounds the guard key as the target key (R031). The limit of a single component is not the limit of the resulting path: for example, a unit name of 1000 bytes gives `testsub/counters/…` of 1017 bytes. There is no common limit of 200 bytes for platform keys. The admissible grammar of components does not guarantee that the full key fits the chosen store; the refusal is `400 badkey` by N4, with no change to the row. The application of this refusal by the unit door is [P/06.1](../../06-console/spec/06.1-console.norms.md) N3–N4, by the worker — [P/03.2](../../03-worker/spec/03.2-worker.norms.md).

## N2 Doors

<!-- req: P/01.1-R023 -->
The client side — the address, the request, the answer forms for the caller, the outcomes and what to do with each, a dropped connection and a replay — is the [store client contract](../../common/contracts/store-client.md); here is the door side.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R204 -->
A process talks to the store of its server over HTTP with JSON bodies through a unix socket.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R205 -->
On a box without a group the store MAY be opened in the process without a door — the profile `file://` «the whole box is one implementation» (P/02.4 N1.2) — but its semantics are the same, and acceptance runs through the door.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R206 -->
Who the caller is, is told by the role socket `/run/configstore/<role>.sock` (P/01.2 N1.1 `identity`).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R024 -->
The door contract is the file [`01-store.api.openapi.yaml`](01-store.api.openapi.yaml) next to this text (OpenAPI 3.1; checked by loading with a tool). Routes: `GET /v1/get?key=`, `GET /v1/list?prefix=`, `POST /v1/write` (`Cmd`: put | delete; `BatchCmd`: batch; `floor` — only on the admin socket of the leader, added by P/01.3 N2); schemas `Key`, `Row`, `Listing`, `Cmd`, `BatchCmd`, `Written`, `Fault`; codes — by N4.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R025 -->
The answer forms are closed: the door MUST return only the fields of the corresponding outcome. `error` is required in a refusal, although acceptance usually does not compare its text. A version in an answer has the form of N1, not of an arbitrary string.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R026 -->
| Answer | Required fields | Optional fields |
|---|---|---|
| a readable row | `items` — an object of strings, `index` — a non-empty version | none |
| an absent row | `items: null`, `index: ""` | none |
| a damaged row | `items: null`, `index` — its non-empty version, `unreadable: true` | none |
| a listing | `keys` — an object key → non-empty version; order by bytes | none |
| a write, a delete or a batch | `index` — the non-empty version handed out (for a batch — one for all its rows) | `again: true` only for a stored replay |
| a refusal | `kind`, `error` and the fields of its N4 row | only `size` in a body refusal by the rule below |

<!-- req: P/01.1-R027 -->
The answer schemas are bound to the code and the operation: for example, a read does not return `ambiguous`, and a `409` cannot have `kind: guard`. A batch `conflict` also carries `key` — the row whose `cas` differed; a single write has no `key`. The size `size > limit`, the actual bytes and the order of `keys` are checked separately: one JSON Schema does not prove these relations.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R028 -->
A damaged row exists and has a version: a listing returns this version (P/01.1-17). A guard compares with the version of a damaged row. A write and a delete with `cas` on the version of a damaged row pass. The version of a row is stored so that damage to the body does not lose it (how — item 5 of «Implementation decides»).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R212 -->
The loss of the version itself is damage to the store, not to the row: a read of this key is 503 `unavailable` (`version_lost`, N4) with a log entry until repaired, not an absence. A listing that touches a key whose version is lost is 503 `unavailable` (`version_lost`, N4) with a log entry until repaired, not a skip. If the current version needed by a put, delete, batch or guard is lost, the operation MUST return 503 `unavailable` (`version_lost`) without its business effect, issuing a version or occupying its `id`. This includes an unconditional put or delete. The check runs when the procedure reads that version, after replay and deadlines; admission metadata follows P/01.1-R070 independently.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R029 -->
The schema `Key` sets the spelling of a key for a read, `Cmd.key` and `guard.key`. The byte limit of N1 is checked separately after encoding in UTF-8.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R030 -->
If `Cmd.key`, `guard.key` or the read parameter `key` is absent or not a string, then the door MUST refuse with `badrequest` (a form violation). If `guard.key` is empty, then the door MUST refuse with `badrequest`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R031 -->
If a string breaks the key rules of N1 — the target key, the read key, or a non-empty guard key — then the door MUST refuse with `badkey` (N4).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R032 -->
Inside `guard` the whole form is checked first — an object, exactly the fields `key` and `index`, their types, the form of `index`, an empty `key` — `badrequest`; and only then the key rules of the guard — `badkey` (`{key: "/x", index: "x"}` — `badrequest`). `guard: null` is out of form, `badrequest`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R033 -->
The elements of `writes` and `guards` of a batch are checked the same way: the `key` of a write and of a guard — form, then key rules. `guards: null` is out of form, `badrequest`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R034 -->
`POST /v1/write` has a separate body read limit — a positive integer number of bytes named in the implementation's description; the limit is not by definition equal to the weight ceiling of `items`. Against the body limit the bytes of the HTTP body are counted, without headers, before JSON parsing. The framing of N2.1 and this limit are the first step of every request of the doors of the store, whatever its method and route: a body larger than the limit is `413 toolarge` (`body_limit`) before the route, the identity and the form of the body. A body of `GET` within the limit is read and discarded; it is not data of the operation. While the body is not larger than the limit, it passes to JSON parsing and the checks of N3. If the body exceeds the limit, then the door MUST refuse with `413 toolarge` before calling the store operation: the row, the version and the `id` memory do not change. `limit` in this refusal is the body limit. `limit` in the refusal for an exceeded row weight is the row ceiling. If the full size of the body is known, the body refusal MAY contain `size` with this size in bytes; if it is unknown, `size` is absent. `key` is not returned in a body refusal: the JSON has not been parsed yet. The acceptance settings are given in N5; the way to set the limit is the implementation's choice.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R040 -->
Extra top-level fields of the command `POST /v1/write` (outside `op`, `key`, `items`, `cas`, `guard`, `not_after`, `not_before`, `id`) are accepted and not read regardless of their JSON type; they do not affect the write. Extra top-level fields of the command `POST /v1/write` are not part of the replay fingerprint. The bytes of extra fields count towards the total size of the HTTP body. The rule of extra fields does not extend to the content of `guard`: its form is closed by N1; `items` is checked by N1 for put and not read for delete by N3.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R222 -->
For `op: batch` the extra top-level fields are those outside `op`, `writes`, `guards`, `id`, `not_after`, `not_before`; among them are `key`, `items`, `cas`, `guard` of a single write: they are accepted and not read. The elements of `writes` and `guards` are closed (N1).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R041 -->
If a route does not exist, then the door MUST answer `404 {kind: badrequest}`. The list of codes and kinds is closed: a new one only by an edit of this lesson.

### N2.1 Request parsing

<!-- req: P/01.1-R042 -->
Parsing is the same in all implementations: on one input they MUST give one code and one refusal kind.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R043 -->
**Query.** The string after `?` is parsed as `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` ([WHATWG URL, urlencoded parsing](https://url.spec.whatwg.org/#urlencoded-parsing)): pairs separated by `&`, name and value by the first `=`. The whole string is parsed by this algorithm. In a name and a value of the query `+` is a space. In a name and a value of the query `%XX` is a byte; a `%` not followed by two hex digits stays as written. Empty pairs of the query (`&&`) are skipped. A parameter without `=` has the value `""`: `?key` gives an empty key, `badkey` (N1).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R225 -->
A client sends a `+` of a key as `%2B`, a `%` as `%25`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R044 -->
**Order of the checks of a read and a listing** (`GET /v1/get`, `GET /v1/list`) when there are several violations — the table [`read-checks.tsv`](../forms/read-checks.tsv): the rows in order, the first violated row gives the code, the kind and the fields of the refusal. The table reads the request after the parsing of this section — the method, the path, the parsed query and the body size — and does not read the store state. After the table [`read-checks.tsv`](../forms/read-checks.tsv) of a read and a listing come the identity and the rights of P/01.2 N3 «check» by the table [`rights-checks.tsv`](../forms/rights-checks.tsv).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R045 -->
If `key` of a read or `prefix` of a listing is repeated, then the door MUST answer `400 badrequest`: the request is out of form. An absent `prefix` is an empty prefix, all rows. An absent `key` of a read is `400 badrequest` (R030).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R046 -->
If the bytes of `key` after decoding are not UTF-8, then the door MUST answer `400 badkey`: a key is a string of N1. `prefix` is bytes after decoding, UTF-8 is not required (N3 «list»).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R047 -->
A body of `GET` is not read as data (only its framing and the body limit, R034). Other query parameters of a read and a listing are not read. A query of `POST /v1/write` is not read. `Content-Type` is not checked.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R048 -->
**The body of `POST /v1/write`** is JSON by [RFC 8259](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8259): a repeated field name in any object of the body (§4) — `400 badrequest`. `NaN` and `Infinity` in the body of `POST /v1/write` — `400 badrequest`. A BOM before the body of `POST /v1/write` (§8.1) — `400 badrequest`. Non-UTF-8 bytes in the body of `POST /v1/write` (§8.1) — `400 badrequest`. A lone surrogate (`\ud800`) in `key` or `guard.key`, or for a batch in the `key` of an element of `writes` or `guards` — `400 badkey`: the string cannot be encoded in UTF-8 nor measured by N1. A lone surrogate in a name or value of `items` — `400 badrequest`. A lone surrogate in extra fields is not read. Numbers and strings of extra fields are not read at any length. The nesting depth is one number for all implementations: `JSON_DEPTH` ([runtime-constants.tsv](../forms/runtime-constants.tsv)) nested objects and arrays from the root, the root object counts 1, in any field, extra ones too; depth `JSON_DEPTH + 1` — `400 badrequest`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R049 -->
A body in `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` is measured by the decoded bytes; exceeding the limit is `413 toolarge` without `size`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R050 -->
**Early refusal.** Before reading the whole body, the door MUST send the complete refusal first, then discard raw HTTP-stream bytes, then close. The early refusal MUST carry `Connection: close`. The door MUST NOT parse or execute the rejected body whose bytes it discards.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R234 -->
Discard stops at the declared body length when framing supplies one. Discard stops earlier at the declared raw-byte limit; this limit MUST NOT exceed the configured `-max-body` value. Discard stops earlier at the absolute monotonic drain deadline. A chunked sender need not send a terminal chunk for discard to finish. The drain volume and time are declared in item 9 of «Implementation decides».

<!-- req: P/01.1-R235 -->
The drain interval starts after the complete refusal has been sent. Incoming bytes do not extend the drain interval.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R236 -->
A second request is not executed after this refusal. A fault response is not added after this refusal.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R237 -->
This shared refusal-before-drain rule also applies at the console ([P/06.5](../../06-console/spec/06.5-console.norms.md)), its bytes door (P/06.1) and the resource (P/05); their own parsing outcomes and response/stream budgets remain with their owners.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R051 -->
**The path** is compared with the routes of N2 byte for byte, without percent-decoding and normalization: `/v1/%67et`, `/v1/get/`, `//v1/get` and a target in absolute form (`http://host/v1/get`) are not routes of N2, `404 badrequest`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R052 -->
**Method and HTTP.** A method or a path outside N2 is `404 {kind: badrequest}` with a `Fault` body, an unknown method on a known path (`PATCH /v1/get`) included. `HEAD` is also `404`, without a body (RFC 9110 §9.3.2).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R239 -->
`Host` is not checked: the door is a unix socket, and an absent, repeated or any value of `Host` is no refusal.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R242 -->
**Request line and headers.** A line ends only with CRLF: a lone LF or CR — `400 badrequest`. Empty lines before the request line — `400 badrequest` (they are not skipped). The request target is only the bytes 0x21–0x7E without `#`; any other byte (a raw `\x00` included) — `400 badrequest`. `%00` in the request target is three admissible bytes: the query key after percent-decoding contains U+0000 and is admissible by N1; the path is compared byte for byte by R051. The header limit is `HEADER_BYTES` ([runtime-constants.tsv](../forms/runtime-constants.tsv)): the sum of the lengths of the field lines without their CRLF and without the final empty line; exactly at the limit — parsed, one byte more — `400 badrequest`. There is no separate limit on the number of header lines.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R054 -->
**Body framing** follows RFC 9112 §6–7: `Content-Length` is digits only (`+5`, a space inside — `400`). Several `Content-Length` with different values — `400` and the connection closed. A chunk size of chunked is HEXDIG only (`0x5` — `400`). A `Transfer-Encoding` other than exactly `chunked` — `400` (`gzip, chunked` and two TE lines too). A length number larger than the implementation can represent — `413 toolarge`: it is certainly larger than the body limit, no `size`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R244 -->
A truncated body — EOF before `Content-Length` or no last chunk of chunked — is not executed: no answer, no write, the `id` memory does not change. After any framing error the door MUST close the connection. The request line, the headers and the body framing are parsed by [RFC 9112](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9112): obs-fold is a parsing violation (§5.2). A space before the colon of a header is a parsing violation (§5.1). With `Content-Length` and `Transfer-Encoding: chunked` together the body is read as chunked (§6.3). A request that does not parse as HTTP/1.1 or HTTP/1.0 (no version, a wrong request line or header, a request line or headers longer than the limit of item 9) is `400 {kind: badrequest}` with a `Fault` body. The codes 414, 431, 501, 505 and default server pages are not in the closed list.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R147 -->
**Input phase deadlines.** The implementation MUST declare finite positive intervals: idle strictly below 60 s, headers strictly below 10 s, body strictly below 30 s, and refusal-drain strictly below 5 s. These intervals are measured by monotonic time.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R248 -->
Idle starts when a connection is accepted or the previous response completes.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R338 -->
The absolute header deadline starts at the first byte of the request line.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R339 -->
The absolute body deadline starts after complete headers have been parsed and establish a body, even if no body byte ever arrives. A slow trickle MUST NOT extend an absolute deadline. Any additional inactivity interval is declared separately.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R250 -->
Before a correct complete request line with a supported HTTP version exists, an input deadline closes the connection without an invented response.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R251 -->
After that line, an input timeout before execution returns `400 BadRequestFault` with `Connection: close` if no response has started; no operation, business write or applied-id record is made. At an input timeout a response that has already started gets no second fault. This timeout rule does not replace R244: EOF or a truncated body still receives no answer, is not executed and does not occupy an id. The phase budgets describe P/01 input, not a common deadline for forwarding, pass or stream responses owned by other lessons.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R055 -->
**Order of command and batch checks** when there are several violations — the table [`write-checks.tsv`](../forms/write-checks.tsv): the rows in order, the first violated row gives the code, the kind and the fields of the refusal. The table reads the body parsed as JSON by this section and does not read the store state (`cas` and `guard` by value, the `id` memory — N3). In every check and status form of this lesson, `#lim` declarations bind parameters by name, not by position. Reordering these declarations MUST NOT change the parameter values or the outcome of the form. Before the table a request of `POST /v1/write` passes, in this order: the frame (the framing of R054 and the body limit, R034 — `413 toolarge`), the route (`404`), the identity of P/01.2 N3 «check» steps 1–2 (a role header on a role socket, the trust of a forwarding — `403`), the JSON form of this section (`400`); `403` by rights is a row of the table. A body larger than the limit with a role header is therefore `413`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R255 -->
The vector package `01-store/write-checks` evaluates the table [`write-checks.tsv`](../forms/write-checks.tsv) by the implementation's own function. Every cell is a string: `sock` is the variable `sock` of the table; `body` is the text of the parsed body, one JSON value; `lim` is the text of a JSON object from the `#lim` names of the table to integers; `allow` is one JSON array text for every row: each element is one JSON string, the text of one `#allow` line after the word `#allow` and one space (`#allow writer read acc/` gives `"writer read acc/"`), in the order of the lines. Each grant has the form `<role> <action or *> [<key prefix>]`, words separated by one space; without a prefix any key is admitted. Only these grants decide `allowed` in this call. The native output of the vector package `01-store/write-checks` is one JSON object: `{"n": 0}` when no row is violated, otherwise `{"n": <the first violated row>, "code": <its code>, "kind": "<its kind>"}`. The column `fault` is not in this output: it belongs to the door. The envelope of the vector package `01-store/write-checks`, package, table, case and cells, is its row of the form [`probe-tables.tsv`](../../common/vectors/probe-tables.tsv), described in the [P/01 write-checks transport](../../common/vectors/wire.md#p01-write-checks-transport). The profile of `01-store` declares the `vectors` entry `01-store/write-checks`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R146 -->
**An integer of a body** is an integer by its notation: `-?(0|[1-9][0-9]*)`, without a fraction and an exponent; `20.0` and `2.0e1` are not integers — a refusal of the form, 400 `badrequest` (P/01.3 N4 `floor`).

## N3 Procedures

<!-- req: P/01.1-R056 -->
The vocabulary in which all procedures of the course are written. An operation is one indivisible action of the store: nobody comes between its steps; whatever comes, comes before or after.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R256 -->
In a group of servers «indivisible» means «one log record applied by every server alike»; on one box — «under one lock» (the implementation decides, see below). A batch is, moreover, indivisible against a power loss: «write batch», «Atomicity».

### read(key) → (items, index)

<!-- req: P/01.1-R057 -->
1. If the key fails the checks of N1, return `badkey`.
2. If the caller has no read right, return `forbidden` (P/01.2 N3 «check»: `allowed(role, read, key)`).
3. If the row `key` does not exist, return (null, "").
4. If the row exists but is not readable (N2), return (null, index, `unreadable: true`).
5. Return (a copy of items, index).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R062 -->
The read MUST be linearizable: it returns a state not older than the last completed write seen by any caller. In a group this means that a read goes through the leader of the current term (a lease renewal is a read, and a follower cut off from the group must not confirm an epoch that the others have already moved; [P/03.2 N3 «renew()» and «confirm(id)»](../../03-worker/spec/03.2-worker.norms.md)). The same guarantee applies to `GET /v1/get` and `GET /v1/list` on a follower's `admin.sock`: they are permitted, without forwarding the admin request (P/01.3-R083). Without confirmation through the current leader within the leader wait, they return `no_leader_wait`, not stale local data; `admin_not_leader` does not apply to these reads. The mechanism — the implementation decides.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R063 -->
**Races.** A read between two writes sees either the first or the second, never a half. There are no others.

### list(prefix) → {key: index}

<!-- req: P/01.1-R065 -->
1. For each row whose key starts with prefix (byte comparison), output its index.
2. Order the keys by bytes.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R066 -->
A listing does not check the read right: it returns all rows under the prefix, including those outside the role's read rights. Identity is checked before the operation by P/01.2 N3 «check»: a substituted role or an untrusted forwarding gives 403 forbidden. For a listing the rows of [`read-checks.tsv`](../forms/read-checks.tsv) (413, 404, 400 of the query) MUST come before the check of identity ([`rights-checks.tsv`](../forms/rights-checks.tsv)), and both before the procedure: a repeated `prefix` with a substituted role gives 400.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R258 -->
`prefix` is any byte string, not a key: the key rules of N1 do not apply to it, the segment `..` means nothing, an empty prefix is all rows. The prefix check gives no 400 except for a repeated parameter (N2.1). A listing does not require read rights to values: it names names and versions, not content. N2 of the door for a listing: 200; 400 of the parsing of N2.1 (a repeated `prefix`); 403 of identity; 404 no route or not `GET`; 413 of the body limit (P/01.1-R034); 503. A prefix SHOULD end with `/`: `testsub/count` would match both `testsub/counters/…` and `testsub/countdown`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R067 -->
**Races.** A write during a listing: the row is either in the list with its version or not; a listing does not block a write. There are no others.

### Admission time

<!-- req: P/01.1-R068 -->
Every log record (put, delete, batch, the service `floor` of P/01.3) gets an **admission time** — Unix seconds:

```
admitted := max(the leader's clock when the record is admitted to the log, the admission time of the previous log record)
```

<!-- req: P/01.1-R070 -->
The leader assigns it when admitting the record to the log and stores it in the log record itself. When a record is **applied**, the conditions `not_after` and `not_before` MUST be checked against this stored time, not against the clock of the applying server: the outcome is one on all servers of the group and does not depend on when the record reached application. The admission time MUST NOT decrease along the log: a new leader and a restarted daemon continue from the admission time of the last record of their log (including one that came by snapshot).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R265 -->
A store without a log — a box (`box` P/01.3 N1.3) and a store in the process — assigns the time by the same rule from its last admission time. Such a store MUST store the admission time as durably as rows: under the same lock and the same durability barrier as the write (item 3 of «Implementation decides»). The admission time is part of the store state and its snapshot (P/01.3 N1.3 `admission`). The copy `copy` of P/01.3 does not carry the admission time: a group after `import` or `restore` starts from the clock of its leader. A write answer does not return the admission time. A leader clock that ran ahead raises the admission time, and writes with `not_after` get `expired` until the clock catches up. An admission time raised by a leader clock that ran ahead is visible by the flag `admission_ahead` (P/01.3 N2 `/v1/status`).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R268 -->
Admission time uses finite binary64 Unix seconds. Convert the clock reading to binary64 with round-to-nearest, ties-to-even before the `max`. The physical clock's accuracy and resolution are not prescribed; conversion to binary64 does not promise to retain a fraction smaller than its representable spacing. The previous admission value and both bounds use the same numeric domain as the converted clock reading (R304). Storage, restoration and application MUST preserve the assigned binary64 numeric value. They MUST NOT additionally round it to integer seconds or milliseconds, or apply the row-writer notation limit of N0. For an admission time A, `not_after` admits A <= bound and refuses A > bound, also on fractional boundaries (N1). For an admission time A, `not_before` admits A > bound and refuses A <= bound, also on fractional boundaries (N1).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R271 -->
An already-admitted replay retains its newly assigned admission time by this rule, even when id memory supplies the first answer without a business-row write, new issued version or changed applied-id entry. The admission time of such a replay must survive an ordinary close/reopen as the last admission metadata. A subsequent rollback of the physical clock cannot undo the admission time of such a replay.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R071 -->
**Races.** A delete with `not_before` = T and a write with `not_after` = T of one row in any order of arrival: the delete is admitted only at an admission time > T; every log record after it has an admission time not smaller, that is > T — `expired`; a write admitted before the delete stands earlier in the log and is removed by the delete (its CAS on the version of the delete will not pass). A common move of wall clocks backwards does not change the order: the admission time does not decrease.

### write(key, items, cas?, guard?, not_after?, not_before?, id?) → index

<!-- req: P/01.1-R072 -->
1. If the key, or the form of cas, guard, id, not_after or not_before, fails the checks of N1, return `badkey` / `badrequest`.
2. If the weight of items exceeds the ceiling, return `toolarge` (nothing written).
3. If the caller has no write right to key, return `forbidden` (P/01.2 N3 «check»: `allowed(role, write, key)`).
4. If guard is given and the caller has no read right to guard.key, return `forbidden`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R076 -->
5. The remaining steps run as one operation, when the log record is applied.
6. Let admitted be the admission time of this record (above, «Admission time»).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R078 -->
7. If id is given and has already been applied:
   1. If that record was different (op, key, items, cas, guard, not_after, not_before), return `badrequest`. The original applied-id entry and its answer stay unchanged by P/01.1-R277; this is after admission, so the new last admission time is retained by R271.
2. Otherwise return its answer with again=true; the rows, issued versions and applied-id entry do not change. The last admission time still advances with its durability by R271 and N3 «Admission time» (R070, R265), even when the answer comes from this memory.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R079 -->
8. If not_after is given and admitted > not_after, return `expired`; nothing is written.
9. If not_before is given and admitted ≤ not_before, return `early`; nothing is written.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R081 -->
10. If guard is given:
    1. Let at be the version of the row guard.key ("" if absent); a lost version follows P/01.1-R212.
    2. If at ≠ guard.index, return `guard` {key: guard.key, index: at}; nothing is written.
11. Let current be the version of the row key ("" if absent); a lost version follows P/01.1-R212.
12. If cas is given and cas ≠ current, return `conflict` {index: current}; nothing is written.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R084 -->
13. Let index be a new version, never handed out before by this store; the order of versions is opaque.
14. Set the row key to (a copy of items, index).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R086 -->
15. If id is given, remember (id → this record, the answer).
16. Return index.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R088 -->
The order of checks inside the operation is normative: the `id` memory, then `not_after` and `not_before`, then the guard, then CAS; with several violations the answer is by the first one. The guard before CAS. A replay of an applied write gets its answer also after `not_after`. A replay that arrived after `not_after` while the first copy was not yet applied gets the answer of the first copy too: the first one is applied first, the replay answers with its answer.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R355 -->
A refusal `expired` and `early`, like `conflict` and `guard`, makes no write and does not take the `id`. The admission time of a record refused with `expired`, `early`, `conflict` or `guard` remains the last admission time (in a group — a log record; a box and a store in the process store it as durably as rows). Only `expired` remains the same refusal for the same deadline and nondecreasing admission time. `early`, `conflict` and `guard` do not occupy the `id`: the same command and `id` are checked again against the current admission time and state and may succeed.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R275 -->
The caller still re-reads its condition and decides anew before another attempt (N4). A guard refusal means a mismatch of the observed version and the absence of this write. The caller re-reads the condition and decides anew whether it has the right to repeat; a version mismatch by itself does not prove a loss of ownership. A CAS refusal also requires a re-read before a new attempt.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R276 -->
A guard on an absent row (`index: ""`) holds while the row does not exist.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R277 -->
The absence of business effects is distinct from admission metadata: an already admitted put or delete that returns `expired`, `early`, `guard`, `conflict`, or `badrequest` for mismatching an already-applied id still updates the last admission time to this record's `admitted` (which may equal the previous time). It does not roll that metadata back with the refused row/id/version effects. This statement concerns an already admitted record, not a request rejected before admission. The admission time so updated is persisted by the same rule as admission in the successful case (N3 Admission time). An id mismatch keeps the first applied-id entry and its original answer, without replacing or freeing them.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R089 -->
A version is handed out only to a completed write: a refusal does not spend a version.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R279 -->
Items are copied on input: the store does not share the memory of the passed items with the caller. Items are copied on output: the store does not share the memory of the returned items with the caller.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R090 -->
The «different record» of a replay is compared by the values of the fingerprint fields, not by the bytes of the body: the order of the fields of `items` and the way JSON strings are escaped have no meaning. In the replay fingerprint `cas: null` equals the absence of `cas`. Both `not_after` and `not_before` of the fingerprint are compared by their finite binary64 numeric values as in N1 (`10` and `10.0` are one), absence equals no number. Distinct JSON number spellings that round to the same binary64 value compare equally and have the same bound fingerprint; exact decimal-rational comparison and raw spelling are not the bound domain. This numeric rule does not apply to versions: they remain exact strings, and `"0"` is not `""`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R091 -->
**Races.** Two writers with one `cas` on one row: exactly one passes, the second gets `conflict` with the version of the first. Two creations with `cas: ""`: exactly one passes.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R094 -->
A write under a guard and a change of the guard (a new capture of the slot) in any order: either the write is made before the change, or it is refused with `guard` and the new version of the guard; there is no state «written after the change».

<!-- req: P/01.1-R095 -->
A replay of a write with the same `id` after an «unknown» answer: if the first one applied, the replay answers with its answer and changes nothing; if not, it applies as the first. After an «unknown» answer the caller does not distinguish these two cases of a replay and must not. A replay with the same `id` but other content (other `items`, `cas` or `guard`): refusal `badrequest`, nothing written — an identifier belongs to one write. A leader change between admission and application: the caller gets `ambiguous`; see above. A write with `not_after` and its replay with the same `id` after `not_after`: if the first one applied, the replay answers with its answer (P/01.1-R088); if not, the replay applies as the first and gets `expired`.

### delete(key, cas?, guard?, not_after?, not_before?, id?) → index

<!-- req: P/01.1-R099 -->
The field `items`, if sent, is not read regardless of its JSON type; its presence is not a refusal.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R100 -->
1. If the key, or the form of cas, guard, id, not_after or not_before, fails the checks of N1, return `badkey` / `badrequest`.
2. If the caller has no delete right to key, return `forbidden` (P/01.2 N3 «check»: never N1.3, `allowed(role, delete, key)`).
3. If guard is given and the caller has no read right to guard.key, return `forbidden`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R103 -->
4. The remaining steps run as one operation, when the log record is applied.
5. Let admitted be the admission time of this record.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R105 -->
6. Replay by id: compare (op, key, cas, guard, not_after, not_before); items is not read and is not part of the
   fingerprint, even if sent with another JSON type.

If they match, return the former answer with again=true.

If they do not match, return `badrequest`.

In either branch the business rows, issued versions and original applied-id entry stay unchanged; the newly admitted time is retained durably by R265, R271, R277, also for a mismatch.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R106 -->
7. If not_after is given and admitted > not_after, return `expired`.
8. If not_before is given and admitted ≤ not_before, return `early`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R108 -->
9. If guard is given, read the version of guard.key by P/01.1-R212; if it differs from guard.index, return `guard`.
10. Let current be the version of the row key ("" if absent); a lost version follows P/01.1-R212.
11. If cas is given and cas ≠ current, return `conflict` {index: current}.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R111 -->
12. Remove the row key (an absent row is no error).
13. Let index be a new version (a delete spends a version).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R113 -->
14. If id is given, remember.

15. Return index.

A delete spends a version so that a stale `cas` on a deleted and re-created row does not pass.

A delete of an absent row is done.

A replay of a done delete is the same delete.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R116 -->
**Races.** As for a write. A delete and a write of one row with one `cas` on an **existing** version: one passes — the delete spends the version. With `cas ""` on an absent row a delete changes nothing (the row was absent and stays absent; the read version stays `""`), and a following write with `cas ""` passes — there is nothing to choose a winner from here.

### write batch(writes, guards?, not_after?, not_before?, id?) → index

<!-- req: P/01.1-R117 -->
1. If the form of the batch fails the checks of N1 (order — N2.1), return `badkey` / `badrequest`.
2. For each put in the order of writes: if the weight of its items exceeds the ceiling, return `toolarge` {key, size, limit} of this row; nothing is written.
3. Rights in the order of writes: `allowed(role, write | delete, key)`; then in the order of guards: `allowed(role, read, key)`;
   if one is missing, return `forbidden` for the whole batch (P/01.2 N3 «check»); nothing is written.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R120 -->
4. The remaining steps run as one operation, when the log record is applied.
5. Let admitted be the admission time of this record (above, «Admission time»).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R122 -->
6. If id is given and has already been applied:
   1. If that record was different (fingerprint below), return `badrequest`. The original applied-id entry and its answer stay unchanged by P/01.1-R277; this is after admission, so the new last admission time is retained by R271.
2. Otherwise return its answer with again=true; the rows, issued versions and applied-id entry do not change. The last admission time still advances with its durability by R271 and N3 «Admission time» (R070, R265), even when the answer comes from this memory.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R123 -->
7. If not_after is given and admitted > not_after, return `expired`; nothing is written.
8. If not_before is given and admitted ≤ not_before, return `early`; nothing is written.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R125 -->
9. For each guard in the order of guards: let at be the version of the row key ("" if absent); a lost version follows P/01.1-R212. If at ≠ the index of the guard, return `guard` {key, index: at}; nothing is written.
10. For each write in the order of writes: let current be the version of the row key ("" if absent); a lost version follows P/01.1-R212, also without cas. If cas is given and cas ≠ current, return `conflict` {key, index: current}; nothing is written.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R128 -->
11. Let index be a new version, never handed out before by this store — one per batch.
12. For each write in the order of writes: put → set the row key to (a copy of items, index); delete → remove the row key
    (an absent row is no error).

<!-- req: P/01.1-R129 -->
13. If id is given, remember (id → this batch, the answer).

14. Return index.

The order is normative: form → weight → rights → the `id` memory → deadlines → guards in
list order → `cas` in list order → application; the answer is the first violation.

A guard on a written key is a form refusal: the condition of such a row is its `cas`.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R132 -->
The refusals `expired`, `early`, `guard`, `conflict` of a batch make no write, do not take the `id` and do not spend versions. The admission time of a batch refused with `expired`, `early`, `guard` or `conflict` remains the last admission time, as for a write. A refusal names the row but not the value: `412 guard {key, index}`, `409 conflict {key, index}` — the version of this row now. The refused batch still updates the last admission time to this record's `admitted` (which may equal the previous time). Zero business-row changes, no occupied `id` and no issued version do not mean rolling back this admission metadata. This applies to each of the four named refusals after admission, not to a request rejected before admission. An admitted batch returning `badrequest` for an id mismatch also retains its new last admission time. This does not apply to a request rejected before admission. An admitted batch returning `badrequest` for an id mismatch has no business-row or issued-version effects; its original applied-id entry and answer remain unchanged (P/01.1-R277). The admission time that a batch keeps has the same durability as admission of a successful batch.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R133 -->
One version per operation: all put rows get the `index` of the batch; a delete spends the same version. A delete of an absent row in a batch is done.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R134 -->
**Replay fingerprint** — `writes` in order (`op`, `key`, `cas`, for put — `items`), the set of `guards`, `not_after`, `not_before`. Values are compared as for a write (R090); the order of the elements of `guards` has no meaning, the absence of `guards` equals `[]` (R189); the order of the elements of `writes` matters; `items` of a delete is not read and not included. The `id` memory is one for writes and batches, its lifetime — the section below, a batch counts as one write: an `id` taken by a put is `badrequest` for a batch, and vice versa.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R135 -->
**Atomicity**. A batch MUST become visible as a whole or not at all: a read and a listing see all its rows or none. After a power loss at any point, a barrier refusal included, the whole batch or none of its rows is visible. The `id` memory of a batch is durable together with it: a replay does not complete half a batch, because there is no half. The `ambiguous` branch of N4 (a medium error, a barrier refusal, a leader change) applies to the whole batch: a replay with the same `id` answers with the first answer or applies the batch as the first. In a group a batch is one log record; the way of a box and of a store in the process is item 3 of «Implementation decides». **Races.** Two batches with a common row and one `cas`: exactly one passes, the second gets `conflict` with the version of the first; none of its rows is written.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R138 -->
A batch under a guard and a change of the guard in any order: either the batch is made before the change, or it is refused with `guard` and the new version; there is no state «part written after the change». Two reads of two rows of a batch are two operations: the first may be before the batch, the second after. One operation sees the batch whole; the caller compares versions: the rows of one batch have one version. A replay with the same `id` together with the first application: as for a write, the first copy is applied first.

### Replay by identifier — store memory

<!-- req: P/01.1-R141 -->
The store MUST answer a replay with the same answer until both conditions hold: more than `ID_REPLAY_MIN_AGE` has passed since the first application **and** at least `ID_REPLAY_MIN_RECORDS` records with an identifier have been applied after it; when both hold, it MAY forget. A forgotten replay is applied as a new write — hence `cas` on every write that matters. The age in the condition runs from the first application; its clock basis is admission time (N3 «Admission time»), because admission time survives a restart; both lower conditions stay.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R150 -->
**Scope: production.** A bounded RAM cache MUST retain access to every still-required replay record through a durable indexed store. Cache eviction MUST NOT forget an ineligible id.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R367 -->
**Scope: production.** Under the qualified declared workload, eligible replay records MUST be collected within the declared finite collection budget. This budget does not weaken either minimum condition of R141. Logical index reclamation and space reuse do not promise an immediate file-size or filesystem-free-space decrease.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R368 -->
**Scope: production.** Memory pressure on the replay storage adds no external refusal and does not turn an uncertain write into a definitely absent one.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R369 -->
**Scope: production.** The teaching store's explicitly named non-durable mode of the replay storage remains distinct from production qualification.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R142 -->
**Races.** Two replays of one `id` at once: one is applied (under the lock or in the log), the second gets the same answer with `again: true`. **Races.** A replay and a new write with the same `id` but other content at once: the earlier one is applied first, the second gets 400.

### Persistence cost

<!-- req: P/01.1-R148 -->
**Scope: production.** Persistence of an ordinary fixed-size write MUST be incremental: each application MUST NOT serialize or rewrite the whole unchanged row store. No particular WAL, engine or consensus algorithm is prescribed by this rule; the library of a group in Go is fixed by [the Raft library rule](../../common/versions/README.md#raft-library-and-implementation-boundary). Checkpoint work of persistence is accounted separately and in the amortized total; a background thread does not hide it. The implementation MUST meet `BUDGET_WRITE_P99`, `BUDGET_WRITE_GROWTH` and `BUDGET_WRITE_BYTES` of the [performance goals](production-acceptance.md#performance-goals) on the declared workload/resource envelope.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R371 -->
**Scope: production.** Import checks the entire immutable input before publication. After this check import builds the state without repeatedly rewriting the already processed prefix. Indexed construction or sorting in O(N log N) is allowed. Under this rule existing versions, the floor, transfer phases and durability barriers are preserved. The implementation declares its cost model and the qualified workload/resources; input bytes and stored-row cardinality are measured separately. The implementation MUST meet `BUDGET_IMPORT_ORDINARY`, `BUDGET_IMPORT_WORST` and `BUDGET_IMPORT_GROWTH` of the [performance goals](production-acceptance.md#performance-goals) on that envelope.

## N4 Refusals

<!-- req: P/01.1-R143 -->
The catalogue of these refusals — [`forms/refusals.tsv`](../forms/refusals.tsv): the form is the sole source of code, kind, fields, headers and log projection. The OpenAPI fault schemas and the orders of checks ([`forms/write-checks.tsv`](../forms/write-checks.tsv), [`forms/read-checks.tsv`](../forms/read-checks.tsv)) stay their owning forms; the causes, priority and caller actions below refer to stable refusal names, without duplicating the outcome cells.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R144 -->
A closed list. The text of `error` is for people and is not normative; the code, `kind` and the fields are.
- `key_rules`: a key not by the rules of N1 (empty, starting with `/`, with a segment `..`, longer than the limit) — the target key; for a guard row — a non-empty key not by the rules (an empty guard key is `badrequest`: out of the form of `guard`); the bytes of a read `key` after decoding are not UTF-8; a lone surrogate in `key` or `guard.key` (N2.1). Caller action: nothing: a code error.
- `body_form`: the body is not a JSON object or not JSON by N2.1 (a repeated field name, `NaN`, `Infinity`, a BOM, not UTF-8, deeper than the limit); `key` or `prefix` repeated in the query; the request does not parse as HTTP (N2.1); `op` is not `put`, `delete`, `batch` or `floor` (`floor` — P/01.3 N2); a lone surrogate in `items`; `key` of a command, `guard.key` or the read parameter `key` is absent or not a string; `items` is not an object of strings for `put`; `cas`, `guard`, `id`, `not_after` or `not_before` out of form (N1); `id` already belongs to another write; a batch out of the form of N1: `writes` absent, empty, not an array or longer than `BATCH_WRITES`, `guards` not an array or longer than `BATCH_GUARDS`, an element not in its closed form, a key twice in `writes` or in `guards`, a guard on a key from `writes`. Caller action: nothing: a code error.
- `no_route`: no route, or no such method on it (N2.1). Caller action: nothing.
- `identity`: the identity is not admitted by P/01.2 N3 «check» (list included): a role header on a role socket, an untrusted forwarding or a forbidden forwarded admin; `op: floor` not on the admin socket (P/01.3 N4). Caller action: fix the identity/trust; the absence of read by itself does not forbid list.
- `rights`: no rights (read, write, delete, guard read). Caller action: nothing: a deployment error or a foreign process (P/01.2 N4, rows «… without permission»).
- `cas_mismatch`: the version of the row is not the one in `cas`. Caller action: re-read, apply your own on top, repeat.
- `expired`: the admission time is later than `not_after` (N3 «Admission time»). Caller action: do not repeat with the same write: the deadline of the write has passed; a new one is decided by the caller by its own norm (an order — [P/03.6](../../03-worker/spec/03.6-worker.norms.md)).
- `early`: the admission time is not later than `not_before`. Caller action: do not repeat with the same write; a new write — at the next step (deleting a row with `made` — [P/04](../../04-controller/spec/04-controller.norms.md), P/03.6).
- `guard_mismatch`: the guard row is not at the version (for a batch — the first in the order of `guards`). Caller action: do not repeat: re-read the slot and check ownership anew ([P/03.2 N3 «write_under_slot»](../../03-worker/spec/03.2-worker.norms.md); the worker — [P/03.4 N4](../../03-worker/spec/03.4-worker.norms.md) «write under slot: `412 guard`»).
- `weight`: the row is heavier than the ceiling (for a batch — the first in the order of `writes`, nothing written). Caller action: nothing: what makes the row heavy goes into an object: a field `type: blob`, the bytes by the door `PUT /<rows>/<id>/<blob_field>` (P/06.1 N1.5, N2).
- `body_limit`: the body is larger than the read limit of N2; the store operation is not called. Caller action: shrink the body; compare its size with the body limit exactly.
- `no_leader_wait`, `lock_wait`, `connection_limit_doors`: no leader was found within the wait time, or the wait time of the local lock expired (item 1 of «Implementation decides»), or there are more connections than the declared limit of the door (`-max-conns` N1.3, a row of N4); the operation is **not done** — for a write, a read and a listing. Caller action: repeat at the next step of the cycle (with `Retry-After` — not before the deadline); hold what you hold.
- `floor_not_higher`: the service `op: floor` on the admin socket (only P/01.3 N2): the new value is not larger than the current one. Caller action: nothing: the lower bound of epochs is already not lower (P/01.3 N1.3).
- `ambiguous_leader`: the write reached the leader, the outcome is **unknown**. Caller action: repeat only with the same `id`; whoever cares — read first.
- `ambiguous_medium`: a medium error before the row is published, and the implementation cannot prove that the write will not apply after a restart (item 7 of «Implementation decides»); if it can — `unavailable`. Caller action: repeat only with the same `id`.
- `ambiguous_barrier`: the durability barrier (item 3 of «Implementation decides») refused when the bytes of the write were already on the medium: the row is published (it will come back after a restart), the outcome for the caller is **unknown** — on one box too. Caller action: repeat only with the same `id`: the replay completes the barriers and answers with the first answer (`again: true`), it hands out no new version; the barrier refused again — `ambiguous` again.
- `input_timeout`: an input phase expires after a correct supported request line and before execution (N2.1). Caller action: correct the pacing or declared input envelope; no applied id or business effect is inferred.
- `version_lost`: a read, listing, put, delete, batch or guard that needs a lost current row version (P/01.1-R212): damage to the store, not to the row; the log names the key. Caller action: repeat at the next step; the operator repairs the store.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R295 -->
A definite pre-effect storage refusal preserves prior business rows, issued versions and applied-id records. An unknown medium outcome stays `ambiguous`; rolling back RAM alone does not prove no effect after recovery.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R296 -->
For `ambiguous_barrier` with a complete recoverable record already on the medium, publish the whole operation with its matching id record. After `ambiguous_barrier` a replay with the same `id` completes the required barriers and returns the first answer with `again`, without a new version. After `ambiguous_barrier` another barrier failure of the replay remains `ambiguous`. A later, distinct write is judged by its own effect: if it is refused before any effect and the store proves it cannot apply after recovery, its outcome is `unavailable`, not `ambiguous` merely because the earlier write is unresolved. If its effect is unknown, it remains `ambiguous`; a successful write requires its durability barriers. This does not require freezing all later writes.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R297 -->
A batch and its applied id recover whole or are absent whole.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R298 -->
Legitimate last-admission changes under admitted refusals/replays are checked separately from business effects.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R145 -->
A refusal MUST NOT quote what the caller sent, except the key: the answer does not grow with a larger error.

<!-- req: P/01.1-R299 -->
A truncated, unreadable or missing answer is on the client side: the [store client contract](../../common/contracts/store-client.md) `no_reply`. A refused connection (an overflowing `accept` queue; there is no separate 503) is on the client side too: `no_reply` of the store client contract. The column «What the caller does» is there too, section «Outcomes and what the client does».

## N5 Acceptance

The scenarios check the requirements named in the readiness checklist below and run through the door of N2 of any implementation by a role with full rights on the prefix `acc/`; the format is in the [acceptance README](../acceptance/1/README.md). The path to the door (a unix socket or a stand with the same door) is a parameter of the runner, not a norm of the lesson.

For this acceptance the runner prepares a door with the body limit **1048576 bytes (1 MiB)** and a store with the row
ceiling **524288 bytes (512 KiB)**. These are fixed parameters of the stand, not a universal body limit of all
implementations. The runner MUST check them by behaviour on a trial instance before the scenarios: a body exactly at the
limit reaches JSON parsing, one byte more — `413` with the body limit in `limit`; a weight of `items` exactly at the ceiling
is written, one byte more — `413` with the row ceiling. How to obtain an isolated instance with these parameters, the
implementation names in its description (item 10 of «Implementation decides»). If the parameters are not provided or the
check does not agree, the run is not performed and does not count as green.
The weight of `items` is checked after body parsing (P/01.1-R072); the body limit refuses before parsing by N2 (P/01.1-R034).
The comparison of the optional `size` in the second case is set in the acceptance README.

An implementation whose store opens in the process without a door (files, memory) SHOULD (not MUST: acceptance norms the behaviour of the door, the internal API is the implementation's choice, item «Implementation decides») run the same scenarios
also through its API, mapping refusals to the codes of N4: so the order «guard before CAS» and the forms are checked in all its stores,
not only behind the door.

Unparseable HTTP (N2.1) is not expressible in TSV: the probe of the acceptance README checks it on raw bytes.

The equality boundary and both fractional sides, and non-decrease on one node at a clock move backwards and a reopen, are checked by process seams for P/01.1-R070, P/01.1-R079, P/01.1-R088, P/01.1-R265, P/01.1-R268, P/01.1-R271, P/01.1-R277. Non-decrease at a leader change, continuation from a snapshot, a replay after `not_after` before the first copy is applied, and `admission_ahead` stay checked by reading:
the door does not move the leader's clock and does not hold a record between admission and application. They are debts of the [probe debt table](../qualification/README.md#probe-debt) (judge-only: qualification procedures).

A power cut in the middle of a batch is checked by reading item 3 of «Implementation decides» and the code: the stand does not cut power. A restart after a batch and its replay, one group log record and forwarding by a door without a vote check P/01.1-R135. A batch body above the limit is the common body refusal before JSON parsing (P/01.1-R034). The row-count exception for `console` is checked under P/01.1-R315.

The following require reading because doors do not reproduce them deterministically: `ambiguous` at the leader's death under P/01.3 N1.3 `leader_wait`; `unavailable` when the local lock wait expires before a write, also the [probe](../probes/README.md) `03-lock-wait`; replay memory by time; linearizability of reads. On the group stand, lack of a quorum gives `unavailable`, and a forwarded write whose answer the leader could not give is `ambiguous` under P/01.1-R144. Leader-death ambiguity stays by reading until its probe appears, as recorded in the [probe debt table](../qualification/README.md#probe-debt) (judge-only: qualification procedures).

The independence of `items` memory on input and output is checked by reading the implementation: the caller's input dictionary and a returned dictionary do not share mutable memory with the store. HTTP serialization hides shared memory: writing and reading a value under P/01.1-R057 and P/01.1-R084 does not prove copying. Running the same two steps through the in-process API without subsequently changing the passed or returned dictionary does not prove copying either.

Production qualification additionally uses the [production acceptance contract](../qualification/README.md) (judge-only: qualification procedures): the independent workload, time, storage/fault and snapshot/output observations are separate from the functional TSV gates. Missing budgets or bindings are `blocked`/`NotExecuted`, not a successful production result.

## Implementation decides

Each implementation names in its description:
1. **the indivisibility mechanism** on one box (a file lock, a mutex) and in a group (a consensus log, in Go over the library of [the Raft library rule](../../common/versions/README.md#raft-library-and-implementation-boundary); a batch is one log record) and the lock wait time — what the caller gets when it expires (as `unavailable`);
2. **how a version is handed out**: a counter in a file, a log index plus the group base — as long as it does not repeat; which number it starts from;
3. **the disk layout** and **the durability barriers**. The order of one write: bytes to the medium, a barrier, publication and the answer; a delete survives a power loss. If the barrier refused when the bytes are already on the medium, the row is published and the answer is `ambiguous` (N4); a replay with the same `id` repeats the barrier. The barriers are not turned off: there is no environment variable and no flag for it, the flag set is closed ([daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv); P/01.3 N1.3 `durability`). **A batch:** how it becomes visible as a whole and after a power loss stays whole or does not appear at all (N3 «write batch», «Atomicity») — an intent log with a barrier before the rows are published, or another way; a lock is not enough: a lock does not survive power, and the rows of different files reach the medium separately;
4. **how a file-based implementation stores every admissible full key** within the file-system name limits;
5. **how a store that can read its row only partially** (a damaged file) reports it: the answer form is a norm (N2 `Row.unreadable`, P/01.1-17); here — how the implementation detects the damage and what it logs, and how it stores the version of the row so that damage to the body does not lose it (the loss of the version — 503, N2);
6. **the replay storage**: its bounded RAM cache, durable index, the retention minimum of N3 and the finite eligible-record collection budget under the qualified workload; the teaching non-durable mode is named separately;
7. **how the door tells «not added to the log» from «unknown»**; the leader wait time before `unavailable` follows the effective `-leader-wait` interval from the loss of the leader, by P/01.3 N1.3; its default is owned only by [daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv).
8. **the read limit of the HTTP body** of a store in the process without a daemon; for the daemon it is set by the flag `-max-body` (the source of defaults is [daemon-flags.tsv](../forms/daemon-flags.tsv)) separately from the row ceiling `-max-value`; the value and meaning of `limit` — by N2, the acceptance parameters — by N5.
9. **the request parsing limits**: the length of the HTTP request line (in bytes, without the final CRLF); exceeding it is `400 badrequest` by N2.1; the length of a chunk-size line of chunked with extensions (without CRLF) — exceeding it is a framing error: `400 badrequest` and the connection closed; the drain volume and time after an early refusal (N2.1). The header limit (`HEADER_BYTES`) and the JSON depth (`JSON_DEPTH`) are norms of N2.1, not a choice; The finite positive monotonic idle/header/body/drain budgets and their exact start events are N2.1; the implementation declares their values and any separate inactivity timer before qualification. Measurement allowance is separate and does not enlarge a protocol deadline.
10. **how the runner obtains an isolated instance** with the acceptance parameters of N5: the start command with substitutions by the section «Stand» of the acceptance README of this lesson — it is common to the acceptance of M10; the lesson does not norm an internal API;
11. **how a listing takes its list** (a copy under the lock, a version snapshot); the guarantee of the «Races» of N3 is kept.
