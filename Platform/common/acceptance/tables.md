# Tables: header, target registry and export lists

A topic of the [common acceptance README](README.md): the directives and doors are its [dictionary](README.md#dictionary-of-directives-and-doors).

## Table header and columns by name

This rule holds for every TSV table whose form names its columns. These are the registries of `forms/` of a lesson and of `common/acceptance/forms/` (among them the [target registry](forms/targets.README.md) and [`targets.routing.tsv`](forms/targets.routing.tsv); judge-only: the routing and the command tables of the judge), [`runner-targets.tsv`](runner-targets.tsv), the vector registries [`wire.tsv`](../vectors/wire.tsv) and [`consumers.tsv`](../vectors/consumers.tsv), and the [command tables](command-tables.md#command-tables) `puts.tsv`, `expect.tsv` and `check.tsv`. A form of a lesson that names its columns follows the rule as well.

1. The table is UTF-8, its lines are separated by LF and its cells by TAB. Empty lines and lines that begin with `#` are notes, except the declarations that a form names (`# cell-json:`, `# canonical:`, `# beside:`).
2. The first line that is not a note is the header. Each of its cells is a column name, and a name occurs once.
3. A reader finds each column by its name in the header, never by its position. A cell means what its column name means in the form. Neither the order of the columns nor an added column changes the meaning of a cell, because a form grows by columns (the column `body` of the routing table) and a reader by position would read a wrong cell after such a change. A column that the form does not name is not read and is not an error, unless the form closes its list of columns.
4. Every other line has exactly as many cells as the header.
5. A table outside this form is a data error of the class `data` (N4 of the [norms](spec/acceptance.norms.md), P/AC-R024; judge-only: the norms of the judge) of every target that reads it, found before the stand starts. Outside the form are: a table without a header, a repeated column name, a missing column that the form requires (a column it lists, unless the form makes it optional) and a line of another number of cells. Such a table is neither a failure of the judge nor an outcome of the implementation.

**Positional forms.** Three forms fix their cells by position and stay outside this rule, because their own forms give the names of the cells and many tables of the lessons are written without a header line:

- the scenario form (judge-only: the scenario tables of the lessons): `scenarios.tsv`, `seams.tsv` and `forms/*.scenarios.tsv` — exactly the four cells `scenario`, `step`, `request`, `expect` (the row of `scenarios.tsv` in the [dictionary](README.md#dictionary-of-directives-and-doors)); the header may be a note;
- the [loader verdicts](command-tables.md#loader-verdicts) of P/02.1: `verdicts.tsv`, `classes.tsv` and `diagnostics.tsv`, with the cells of that form and without a header;
- a vector case table whose cell `header` in `wire.tsv` begins with `-`: it has no header line, and the names after `-` name its cells by position ([vector wire form](../vectors/wire.md)).

## Target registry and export lists assembled from the lessons

The target registry and the two filters of the export are each assembled from a common part and the parts of the lessons. A lesson is a directory `Platform/NN-<process>/`. The export, `check_spec`, the cycle and the engine read only the assembled registry and the assembled filters, never a common file or a file of a lesson alone.

1. **Registry.** The common files [`forms/targets.tsv`](forms/targets.tsv) and [`forms/targets.routing.tsv`](forms/targets.routing.tsv) (judge-only: the routing of the judge) hold the header of each table and, in notes, the meaning of its columns ([target registry](forms/targets.README.md)); they hold no rows. The rows of the targets of a lesson are in `forms/targets.tsv` and `forms/targets.routing.tsv` of that lesson (judge-only: the routing of the judge), with the required columns of the common header; the optional `execution` column of routing may be omitted and then supplies `{}`. Routing columns are read by name and projected to the common header on assembly; unknown columns are ignored. The public target table keeps its common header; notes are allowed ([table header and columns by name](#table-header-and-columns-by-name)). The assembled table is that header followed by the rows of the lessons in the order of their directory names, the rows of each file in their order. An `id` occurs once in the assembled table, a row of a lesson has the `owner` of that lesson, and the routing row of a target is in the same lesson as its target row.
2. **Lists of the files of a lesson.** The model filter `Platform/common/export-model-filter.json` (M) and the judge input filter [`judge-input-filter.json`](judge-input-filter.json) (J) (judge-only: the filters of the export) give one record to every file of the export: `path`, `disposition` and `basis`, sorted by `path`. The records of the files of a lesson are in its form `forms/export.json`: a JSON object with exactly the fields `version` (the integer `1`), `model` (the records of M) and `judge` (the records of J), each array sorted by `path` as UTF-8 bytes. A list names every file of its lesson directory, `ru/` and `journal/` among them, once in `model` and once in `judge`, and no other file. A common filter keeps the records of the files outside the lesson directories and of the files of a lesson without `forms/export.json`; a path has one record in one place.
3. **Common rules of the lists.** In every lesson `forms/targets.tsv` is `model_and_judge` in M and `include` in J, as the common public table; `forms/targets.routing.tsv` (judge-only: the routing of the judge) is `judge_only` in M and `include` in J, as the common routing table; `forms/export.json` is `judge_only` in M and `exclude` in J, as the common filters.
4. **Assembled filter.** The assembled M filter is the common M filter whose `files` are its own records and the records of `model` of the lists of all lessons, sorted by `path` as UTF-8 bytes; its other fields are those of the common file. The assembled J filter is the same with the common J filter and `judge`. An assembled filter is written as JSON with an indent of two spaces, characters outside ASCII as they are, LF line ends and a final LF; these are its bytes wherever a blob of the filter is bound.
